# Wåndyr — Approaches

## A Variant for Wåndyr, an Adventure Game

**Approaches replace Alignment.** Rather than tracking individual Alignments separately, all advancements sort into four Approaches — **Sword, Craft, Heart, and Arcana** — each calculated exactly like Alignment Level: count relevant advancements, divide by three, round down.

The four Approaches are organized by two questions:

**Is it violent?** If yes — Sword, regardless of sword or sorcery, regardless of time.

**If not violent — does it take time?** If no — Craft. If yes — Heart if oriented toward others, Arcana if reaching beyond the ordinary world.

---

## The Four Approaches

**Sword** — violent confrontation, regardless of sword or sorcery. The melee, the duel, the brawl, combat spells, Hold Person cast in a fight. Immediate by nature — violence doesn't wait. Your Sword level provides Iron hit points for yourself or an adjacent companion during combat. Maps to Spades.

**Craft** — non-violent worldly skill, at any pace. Fast or slow, urgent or patient — if it's practical capability without violence, it's Craft. The pickpocket's quick hand and the blacksmith's slow forge are both Craft. Lockpicking, hunting, music, scouting, deception, the patient shot, the quick dodge, the thrown rock as distraction. Your Craft level cancels hit points lost to fatigue for the group during overland travel. Maps to Clubs.

**Heart** — takes time, oriented toward others. The restorative meal, the overnight healing, the blessing before a journey, the long march sustained by quiet giving, the saint who channels grace for companions. Sam has Heart. The sherpa has Heart. Your Heart level is the number of hit points you may donate to companions during a session. Maps to Hearts.

**Arcana** — takes time, reaching beyond the ordinary world. Deep magical knowledge, communing with otherworldly beings, divination, slow curses and hexes, reality-reshaping magic. A Magic Missile in combat is Sword. A curse prepared over three days is Arcana. The spell doesn't determine the approach — the situation, the intent, and the time frame do. Your Arcana level determines maximum spell level, maximum Arcana items carried, and therefore maximum Arcana hit points available for casting. Maps to Diamonds.

---

## Sorting Existing Alignments

- Magic school Alignments → **Arcana**
- Fighting style Alignments → **Sword**
- Otherworldly being Alignments → **Heart** or **Arcana** depending on the nature of the relationship
- Faction Alignments → whichever Approach fits the faction's nature

---

## Approach Level

Calculate exactly like Alignment Level: count relevant advancements, divide by three, round down.

*Example: A character with the Traits "fierce" and "deadly", the Skills "greatsword" and "armor", and the Byname "Trollbane" has 5 advancements relevant to Sword — that's Sword level 1.*

*Example: A character with the Traits "charming" and "bard", the Skills "lute" and "Canting", two Songs, and a Recipe has 6 advancements relevant to Craft — that's Craft level 2.*

---

## Muster

At Muster, optionally assign one **Trait** to each Approach — how you play that Approach today. Optionally assign one **Skill** to one Approach — your specialization, worth +1 on relevant rolls. Bynames may substitute for either.

The Trait colors the fiction. The Skill sharpens the mechanic. Both are optional.

---

## The Shadow Approach

The Shadow Approach is the one with the most complicated relationship to who you are — a longing, a wound, a blind spot, or a surprise. It cannot advance beyond zero through normal play. Only a transformative narrative moment the whole table recognizes can open it further.

The Shadow is not a weakness or a penalty. It is the most interesting thing about the character.

*Bilbo's Shadow Approach is Sword — not a longing, a surprise. He didn't know he could be that until Sting was in his hand in the dark.*

*Elric's Shadow Approach is Heart — destroyed by Stormbringer repeatedly until he stopped trying. Heart 0 is not absence of feeling. It is learned helplessness in the face of a curse.*

*The Grey Mouser's Shadow Approach is Heart — closed by grief after Mistling. He loves Fafhrd but it isn't Heart. It's a partnership of mutual survival. The door closed when Mistling died and never fully opened again.*

*Conan's Shadow Approach is Arcana — active rejection, not longing. Magic is the thing that unmans him. He destroys it when he can.*

## The Oracle Roll

> *2d6 + Approach Level + Skill bonus (0 or +1) + Guide modifier vs. 8*

The target is always 8. The Guide applies a modifier based on difficulty — reacting naturally to what the player describes. The modifier is typically negative for difficult situations and positive for favorable ones. The player reads their Approach Level from their sheet. No fiat required.

**Target 8 on 2d6 produces approximately 58% success on an unmodified roll** — deliberately calibrated to the research-backed sweet spot of 60% player success. Every point of Approach level offsets one point of negative modifier, keeping the sweet spot accessible for appropriate challenges.

Equal challenge, even odds, always — a Sword 3 character facing a −3 modifier rolls 2d6 unmodified, just as a Sword 1 character facing a −1 modifier does.

---

## Guide Modifiers

The Guide applies modifiers based on feel — reacting to the fiction rather than looking up numbers:

| Difficulty | Modifier |
|---|---|
| Straightforward | 0 |
| Slightly tricky | −1 |
| Hard | −2 |
| Very hard | −4 |
| Extreme | −6 |
| Nearly impossible | −8 |
| Favorable conditions | +1 or +2 |

**For spells**, the modifier is the higher of the spell level or the creature's magic resistance — not both combined:

- Casting a 3rd level spell at a townsperson → −3 (spell level only)
- Casting a 3rd level spell at a Wraith → −5 (Wraith's resistance exceeds spell level)
- Casting with hands partially bound → Guide may add −1 on top

The Guide may stack modifiers from multiple sources when the fiction demands — but should do so sparingly and always with a clear fictional reason.

## Oracle Outcomes — Sweet, Spicy, and the Oracle Tradition

The Oracle tradition predates Wåndyr. Its four voices are:

| Oracle voice | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **Yes, and** | Success with something extra |
| **Yes** | Clean success |
| **Yes, but** | Success with complication |
| **No, but** | Failure — but something good happens, or the player may push through at cost |
| **No** | Clean failure |
| **No, and** | Failure with additional consequence |

Wåndyr expresses these through two independent dimensions evaluated separately:

**Dimension 1 — Did you meet the Oracle target?** Success or failure.

**Dimension 2 — What faces are showing?**

- A **6** showing on either die — **Sweet**
- A **1** showing on either die — **Spicy**
- Both a **6** and a **1** showing — **Sweet and Spicy simultaneously**

These two dimensions combine to produce the full outcome:

| | Sweet (6 showing) | Plain | Spicy (1 showing) | Sweet & Spicy (6 and 1) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Success** | Clean Sweet | Success | Spicy Success | Clean Sweet Spicy |
| **Failure** | Dirty Sweet | Failure | Spicy Failure | Dirty Sweet Spicy |

---

## The Six Outcomes Explained

**Clean Sweet** — you met the target AND a 6 is showing. *Yes, and.* Something extra and wonderful happens on top of success. The Guide announces it.

*"Clean Sweet — tell me what happens."*

**Success** — you met the target, no special faces showing. *Yes.* Clean, straightforward success.

**Spicy Success** — you met the target but a 1 is showing. *Yes, but.* Something goes wrong even as you succeed. The Guide introduces a complication.

**Dirty Sweet** — a 6 is showing but you fell short of the target. *No, but.* The narrative happens anyway — automatically, no decision required.

A Dirty Sweet automatically succeeds. Lose hit points equal to the non-6 die. If that would put you below 1 hit point, stay at 1 hit point — fortune favors the bold!

**Failure** — you fell short, no special faces showing. *No.* Clean failure.

**Spicy Failure** — you fell short AND a 1 is showing. *No, and.* Failure with additional consequence. Something bad happens on top of failing.

**Clean Sweet Spicy** — you met the target with both a 6 and a 1 showing. *Yes, and... but.* Something wonderful happens and something goes wrong simultaneously. The most chaotic successful outcome in the system.

**Dirty Sweet Spicy** — you fell short with both a 6 and a 1 showing. *No, but... and.* The player may push through at cost AND something bad happens on top of that. The most complicated outcome in the system — rare at approximately 1 in 18 rolls.

---

## The Dirty Sweet in Play

The Dirty Sweet appears on approximately **30.6% of all rolls** — roughly 1 in 3. It is an escape valve that keeps the game fun and possible against brutal modifiers. When the Guide says "roll at −8" you still have a shot.

A Dirty Sweet automatically succeeds. No decision point. No pause. The narrative happens and the cost is immediately visible on the table — the non-6 die tells everyone what was paid.

**The cost scales naturally:**

- Rolled 6 and 1 — succeed, lose 1 hit point. Cheapest possible. Also Spicy.
- Rolled 6 and 3 — succeed, lose 3 hit points.
- Rolled 6 and 5 — succeed, lose 5 hit points. Near miss but costly.
- Rolled 6 and 6 — succeed, lose 6 hit points. Maximum cost. Double 6 against a brutal modifier — the most desperate success in the game.

If the cost would drop you below 1 hit point, stay at 1 hit point — fortune favors the bold!

**The Bilbo examples:**

Bilbo killing the spider — Dirty Sweet, 6 and 4. The spider dies. Bilbo loses 4 hit points. He's trembling, badly stung, barely standing. But the spider is dead.

Sam driving off Shelob — Dirty Sweet, 6 and 3. Shelob retreats. Sam loses 3 hit points. Exhausted and wounded. But Frodo is saved.

Bard loosing the Black Arrow — Dirty Sweet, 6 and 6. Smaug falls. Bard loses 6 hit points — the maximum cost, reduced to 1 hit point if needed. The last arrow. Everything spent. The dragon is dead.

**At the table:**

*Player rolls 6 and 3. Total plus modifiers: 7. Target: 8.*

Guide: *"Dirty Sweet — the lock opens but the guard hears something. Lose 3 hit points."*

Game continues.

## Approach Gifts

Each Approach provides a gift scaled to its level, connecting directly to existing Wåndyr mechanics.

| Approach | Gift | When |
|---|---|---|
| **Sword** | Iron hit points for self or adjacent companion | Combat |
| **Craft** | Cancel fatigue hit points for the group | Overland travel |
| **Heart** | Donate hit points to companions | Anytime |
| **Arcana** | Arcana hit points — one per Arcana item carried, up to Arcana level | Spellcasting |

## Sword-and-Sorcery Characters

| | Sword | Craft | Heart | Arcana |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Conan** | 4 | 3 | 2 | 0 Shadow — fear |
| **Fafhrd** | 3 | 3 | 3 | 0 Shadow — incomprehension |
| **Grey Mouser** | 3 | 3 | 0 Shadow — grief | 2 |
| **Elric** | 3 | 1 | 0 Shadow — learned helplessness | 4 |

Fafhrd and the Grey Mouser share identical Sword and Craft levels — Leiber wrote them as equals in combat and practical capability. The difference between them lives entirely in Traits and Skills at Muster.

### Conan of Cimmeria

*Muster — a day of battle:*

- Trait "Barbarian King" → Sword
- Trait "Cimmerian" → Craft
- Skill "his broadsword" → Sword (+1)

### Fafhrd of the Cold Waste

*Muster — a day of adventure:*

- Trait "Giant Swordsman" → Sword
- Trait "Northerner" → Craft
- Skill "his climbing" → Craft (+1)

### The Grey Mouser

*Muster — a day of thieving:*

- Trait "Consummate Swordsman" → Sword
- Trait "Master Thief" → Craft
- Skill "Scalpel" → Sword (+1)

*Fafhrd and the Mouser at the same Muster — same levels, completely different characters:*

Fafhrd assigns "Giant Swordsman" to Sword and "Northerner" to Craft. He is the wanderer who fights with reach and power, whose Craft is wilderness and physical. The Mouser assigns "Consummate Swordsman" to Sword and "Master Thief" to Craft. He is the thief who fights with precision and speed, whose Craft is urban and technical. Same numbers. Completely different people.

### Elric of Melniboné

*Muster — a day of sorcery:*

- Trait "Sorcerer-Emperor" → Arcana
- Trait "Last Emperor" → Sword
- Skill "Stormbringer" → Sword (+1)

*Muster — a rare day when he lets someone in:*

- Trait "Sorcerer-Emperor" → Arcana
- Trait "I Would Be Otherwise" → Heart — the Shadow Approach Trait, the impossible wish
- Skill "Stormbringer" → Sword (+1)

That second Muster is the most dangerous one at the table. Every time Elric assigns a Trait to Heart, Moorcock kills someone.

Use a standard deck of cards at Muster. Lay your suit card face up — the Five of Spades, the Three of Hearts — to show your primary Approach and level at a glance.

| Approach | Suit |
|---|---|
| Sword | Spades |
| Craft | Clubs |
| Heart | Hearts |
| Arcana | Diamonds |

Face cards — Jack, Queen, King — may be awarded by the table as recognition of legendary status when a character reaches the pinnacle of their Approach. This is entirely optional and adds nothing mechanical.

## Guide Notes

**Ask the question.** "How do you go about that?" is one of the most powerful questions at the table. It invites the player to declare their Approach before a single die is rolled.

**The target is always 8.** Apply a negative modifier for difficulty. The player adds their Approach Level and Skill bonus. The math is visible to everyone. No fiat required.

**Modifiers are motivated.** Always give a reason — "that's hard, −2" or "the Wraith resists, −5." The modifier should emerge from the fiction, not appear from nowhere.

**Approaches are not situation-locked.** Sword is not only for combat. A tournament champion might invoke Sword to calm a crowd. A person of genuine Heart might invoke Heart to negotiate safe passage. The fiction determines the Approach.

**The Shadow Approach is a story, not a penalty.** Treat it as the most interesting thing about the character — the thing they reach for and cannot fully become, or the thing that surprises everyone including them.

**Heart donations are gifts, not transactions.** When a player donates hit points through their Heart level, it is a fictional act of generosity. The mechanic should emerge from the fiction, not precede it.

**Dirty Sweet is automatic.** When a player rolls a Dirty Sweet the narrative happens — no decision point, no pause. The cost is the non-6 die, visible immediately on the table. The Guide narrates the messy success and the game continues.

**Plan A is always player cleverness — no dice needed.** When a player describes what they do with enough specificity and intelligence that the outcome isn't in doubt, the Guide says yes and the fiction moves forward. No roll required. "I look behind the tapestry for a hidden door" — yes, you find it. "I pour oil on the stairs before they charge" — yes, the goblins slip. Player skill is rewarded directly. Approaches only come into play when genuine uncertainty exists despite good thinking. The Guide's most important question before calling for a roll is — has the player already solved this through clever description? If yes, say yes and move on.

**Approaches are Plan B and onwards.** Sword covers violent confrontation — regardless of sword or sorcery. Craft covers non-violent worldly skill at any pace. Heart covers time-available action oriented toward others. Arcana covers time-available action reaching beyond the ordinary world, including non-immediate magical violence like curses and hexes. If the Guide is calling Sword, something has gone wrong upstream. The goal is always to solve problems before they become Sword situations. This is The Hobbit — not soldiers on a military campaign, but wanderers who persevere, improvise, and soldier on. Most characters should be Craft-primary. Sword should feel like a last resort.

**Magic follows the situation, not the spell.** The Approach for a magical action is determined by violence, time, and intent — not by the spell itself. A Magic Missile in combat is Sword. A Hold Person cast mid-fight is Sword. A curse prepared over three days is Arcana. A healing spell cast at camp is Heart. A magical disguise crafted with time and skill is Craft. The same spell school can produce actions across all four Approaches depending on how and when it is used. A wizard who wants to be effective with combat spells needs Sword advancements — martial grounding, combat awareness, the steadiness to cast under pressure — not more spells.

**The Guide never rolls Approaches.** Approaches belong to the players. Monsters and obstacles have modifiers, not Approaches.
