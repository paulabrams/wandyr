# Wåndyr 1.5 — Unified Resolution (Experiment)

*Experimental. Not in effect. A proposed rewrite of the core resolution, combat, and magic engines around a single idea: **Hearts are the economy, and every roll is a price.** Supersedes the partial "Core Resolution (DRAFT)" where they conflict.*

---

## The Premise — "I DO this"

You declare what your character does in the *doing*, not the attempting. **"I climb the wall,"** not "I'd like to try the wall."

Wåndyr runs on two engines, and keeping them apart is the whole trick:

- **The World Engine** — how the world behaves on its own terms. The Guide owns it. People, monsters, doors, and pits have their own nature, danger, and limits, and these do **not** bend because a character wishes it. The Guide drives it with rulings, random tables, and the Oracle.
- **The Hero Engine** — how *you* act in that world. You own it. You say what you do, and roll for what it **costs.**

The deepest principle: **player skill decides *whether* the world bends; character power decides only *how much effect* you have.** A good idea, well described, makes things happen. Your sheet makes them happen *harder* — it never makes the impossible possible.

---

## The Two Steps

Every action resolves in at most two beats.

### Step 1 — Say What You Do

The Guide listens and rules:

- **Clever and feasible → it just works.** No roll. You rope the cross-beam and swing the pit; you feel the lintel and find the tripwire.
- **No chance → it just fails.** A fist won't break an oak door; a loyal jailer won't free the prisoners he's posted to guard.
- **Feasible but risky or uncertain → go to Step 2.**

### Step 2 — Pay the Cost

A risky deed doesn't *fail* — it has a **Cost.** A Cost is anything that diminishes Hearts: a fall, a blow, a trap, the strain of a spell or feat. The Guide quotes the Cost; you roll to reduce or deal it.

*It's ordering at a counter.* You say what you want; the Guide quotes the price; you pay.

> **Player:** "I leap the pit." — **Guide:** "Narrow one — 3 Hearts." *(Roll; you'll likely clear it free.)*
>
> **Player:** "I charge in and grab the treasure." — **Guide:** "Pressure plates — darts! 12 Hearts." *(You committed; now roll. The quote is how the world springs its teeth — and a careful player who'd checked the floor pays nothing.)*
>
> **Player:** "Large coffee, black." — **Guide:** "$4." *(Same loop.)*

You *always* get the thing. The dice only decide what it took out of you. **Failure is Cost.** Where Hearts don't fit the fiction, the price can be **time, noise, position, or a Spicy twist.**

### No Roll Is Free

> **No roll without a named Cost. No named Cost without a roll.**

The dice come out only once a price is on the table, so **the dice are the line** between Plan A (cleverness, free) and Plan B (the roll, costs). Every roll, *someone* pays — and the **dread is when it's you.** That single asymmetry is what stops the reflexive "I just roll": cleverness is the toll-free road.

You roll for one of two reasons, and they're independent:

| | **Safe** (no Cost to you) | **Dangerous** (a Cost to you) |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Certain** | Just happens. No roll. | Pay the toll — roll only to soften a *certain* Cost (charge the fire to reach the lever: you arrive, but you burn). |
| **Uncertain** | Roll for **Effect** — how well, no danger (the far arrow: it hits; how hard?). | Roll, and bleed — Effect *and* exposure (melee; the wide pit). |

An **Effect** roll prices the world or the enemy (you want it high). A **Cost** roll prices *you*. Same dice; what differs is whether your *glory* or your *Hearts* are on the line.

## The Roll

> **Result = 2d6 + Alignment + (a 3rd die if the implement is *bonded*).**

The **sum is the magnitude** — your damage, your reduction, your effect. **Alignment** (your relevant level, advancements ÷ 3) adds flat: this is your character power, and it grows as you level.

**A plain 2d6 caps at 12 — the mortal ceiling.** Alignment and the 3rd die are how you climb past it.

### Sweet & Spicy

A **6** is **Sweet** (a good twist); a **1** is **Spicy** (a complication). They **ride along independently and never change the sum** — a [1,6] is a 7 carrying *both* a flourish and a complication. Tally them:

- One = a twist. **Two of a kind = Double** (Double Spicy is the catastrophe — ask the Oracle something dire). **Three of a kind** (only possible on a bonded 3-die roll) = a legendary extreme.
- More dice = more chaos. A bonded/magic roll (3 dice) throws twists far more often (≈42% vs 31% each way) — power is *wild*.

---

## The 3rd Die — Bond, Not Bonus

There are **no "+1/+2" plusses anywhere.** Instead, a deep bond between you and an implement grants a **third die** (binary — you have it or you don't; never a fourth). The bond can be:

- An **enchanted** item (magic weapon, shield, armor).
- A **named** item — one you've earned a **Byname** for through a deed. *The legend is the magic;* "Trollbane" rolls 3 dice like any enchanted blade.
- A **mastered spell** (a *known* spell) cast through its **book or Arcana.**

**Consequences:**

- **No plus-treadmill.** You never trash a beloved blade for a bigger number — all bonded weapons are equal (3 dice). You keep the one whose *vibe and story* you love.
- **Items differ by *feature*, not number** — flaming, holy, demon-bane, silent. You choose by **identity and fit.** "Legendary" means a more *wondrous feature* (or a sentient soul, or a Relic), never a bigger bonus.
- **It's fiction-gated.** The 3rd die applies only when the implement plausibly fits the action. A magic **shield** parries a fire breath (broad, raised → 3 dice); a magic **sword** does not (a blade can't shield fire); a magic **breastplate** covers the torso but *your face still burns.* "Does this help against *that*?" is a Step-1 ruling.
- **Arcana is the caster's power-item** — the fighter's named blade, for a mage. It's the always-carried **focus** that unlocks the 3rd die on mastered spells (from bare memory = 2 dice; through your Arcana = 3), it holds **Arcana Hearts** (casting + anti-magic pool), and it's portable wizard-bling over books.

You grow by **mastering, naming, and bonding** — and by Alignment, Bynames, and Knacks — not by chasing numbers. Your blade and your Arcana walk the whole road, there and back again.

## Hearts — the One Economy

Everything that diminishes Hearts is a **Cost.** At **0 Hearts you're out — and the *cause* flavors the out-state:**

- Sword / fire → **dying** (offscreen, possibly returnable via Invocation; or a heroic onscreen last act).
- Charm / dominate → **enthralled.** Maze → **lost.** Fear → **fled.** Petrify → **stone.** *(Control out-states are recoverable — never death.)*

**There is no save-or-die coin flip.** Death comes from **damage exceeding your Hearts** — HP-mediated, mitigable (defend, armor, don't be there), and earned. A big enough blow *can* drop you in one hit (a 3d6 nuke vs a 7-Heart body), but only when **undefended** (surprise, an un-dodgeable area); a defended hit is survivable. The dice never say "you die" — the *price* does.

## Fighting

A round is a minute. You get **one action** (attack, cast, a feat). **Defending is free and unlimited** — a minute is long enough to parry many things.

Everything in a fight has **Hearts** (life), maybe **Iron Hearts** (armor — a buffer soaked before real Hearts), and a **defense** the attacker's roll is measured against.

- **You attack:** *"I cut the orc — roll — 7."* Damage = your roll + Alignment **− their defense**, to Iron Hearts then Hearts.
- **You defend:** the incoming **Pow** is a Cost; roll to reduce it (take **Pow − your roll**), soaked by Iron Hearts. Armor covers only what it covers (a breath cooks the gaps).

**Exposure — why attacking costs.** An attack is *unsafe* when the foe can answer it. **Melee** is mutual — fast and furious, you trade blows. **Range and reach buy safety** (a longer weapon lands first; a far arrow exposes you to nothing until they close; a sniped, unaware sentry is fully safe — its only Cost is being *noticed*). Only the unsafe is dreadful, so a wise party softens foes at distance and *fears the melee.*

**Tension lives in three places**, not a to-hit: **volume** (one kill per round, many attackers = many parries, the low tail adds up), **big hits leak** (a 2d6 parry can't fully turn a high Pow), and **position** (surprised, prone, or swarmed → defend at Disadvantage).

**Tactics move the Target.** Pow, Tou, and Level belong to the *situation*:

- A shield-wall is **Tou 6 from the front, Tou 2 from the flank.**
- A **swarm combines into one higher Pow** (six clubbers = Pow 6, a dozen = Pow 10). Kill them and the combined Pow drops each round — which is why you attack, not just turtle.
- A **chokepoint caps the combinable Pow** (funnel six orcs to a doorway and they're back to Pow 2 — Thermopylae for free).
- **Flank, high ground, cover, surprise** — all just the Guide nudging Pow or Tou. **Always narrate the cause** so players can attack the *reason.*

## Monsters

**A monster is a PC with the dice pre-rolled.** It needs one number — its **Level** — and everything follows:

- **Defense** = its Level (its skill at not being hit / hurt). Use the existing Level table (rat 0 … dragon 10).
- **Pow** (its attack) = the **flat average** of `2d6 + bonus` (or 3 dice if it wields something bonded). The Guide **rolls nothing** for monster offense — narrate the Pow, the player defends.
- **Iron Hearts** = natural armor (hide, scales), exactly like a PC's.
- **Hearts** = life (pack creatures few; an apex predator ≈ 2d6 per Level).
- **Tou** is the same idea for **inanimate** things (a door doesn't dodge — Oak door, Tou 12).

### The Apex Gate

Because a plain 2d6 caps at 12:

- A foe whose **Pow tops 12 cannot be fully parried** — you survive only on Hearts and a *magic* (3-die) defense.
- A foe whose **defense reaches ~12 cannot be hurt by a mundane 2d6 at all** — you need the **3rd die** (a bonded weapon) or high **Alignment.**

Reserve the gate for true apex/mythic foes. *(Session 57: the Kalla demon fell to the **named/holy Alu sickles** — the 3rd die clearing the gate — plus Tyrion's entangle pinning its defense and Rook's backstab striking where its defense didn't apply.)*

## Magic

You cast by naming the spell and what it does (Step 1). Then pay the **Spell Level in Hearts** (Arcana Hearts first), capped by your Alignment in that school. **Spell Level buys *scope*** — area, targets, range, duration, the control threshold — **not single-target punch.** A fireball is mighty because it hits *everyone*, not because it hits one harder.

Magic comes in two modes, both denominated in Hearts:

### Damage magic

`2d6 (3 if mastered through book/Arcana) + Alignment`, **reduced by the target's defense/resistance (DR)**, to **current Hearts.** Cumulative attrition, the compressed grind. Low-level damage is a *bad trade* (it loses to a free weapon swing), so a mage's low magic is **utility and control**, and damage-casting is for *scope* (hit the room).

### Control magic — charm, beguile, dominate, maze, hold

Reduces Hearts **like damage, but ignoring DR — you can't armor a mind.** If your spell brings the target to **0 (your roll ≥ its current Hearts), it *grabs hold*** — subverted, *flipped*, enemy-into-friend (recoverable, **not** dead). Short of 0, it just **chipped.**

So control **removes the weak**, **finishes the battered** (the team's siege lowers the bar), and **only chips the fresh strong** (a 22-Heart boss can't be flipped in one cast). To *grab* rather than kill, your spell must land the zero-blow — so the caster calls *"leave it for me!"* and times the finish.

**The choice between Charm and Finger of Death is about the *outcome you want*, not the math** — same Hearts engine, different door at zero: dead, yours, lost, stone, banished, fled. *One engine, many zero-doors.*

**Overrides (Guide rulings, not a tracked pool):**

- **Mind-immune** — a golem or mindless thing can't be controlled at all.
- **Mind-resistant** — an alien or iron will (a demon, a lich) needs a **Sweet** or a high roll to bite (this is why Session 57's Mind Maze "took a Sweet").

The mage is the party's **ceiling-breaker** — the 3rd die (mastery + Arcana) and high Alignment let magic climb past the mortal 12.

## Lingering Costs — Poison, Petrification, Paralysis

These are **not** save-or-die — they're a **deadly countdown.**

- **Defend the bite** with a roll (a clean defense can avoid envenomation entirely).
- Once it lands: a **flat Cost per round for HD/Level rounds** (e.g., a spider's venom, 8/round for 3). It's **internal** — no per-round dodge — and **bypasses armor.** The only outs are a **cure** (antidote, Vitae) or surviving the duration.
- **0 Hearts → dead** (or *petrified / paralyzed* — the cause flavors the out-state, recoverable).

Zero Guide rolls (flat damage, flat duration), full dread (watch the Hearts drain, race for the cure). Reserve big per-round numbers for foes that earn the terror.

## Area Effects & Warding

An **AoE** (a dragon's breath) is the *un-dodgeable* one-shot. Its counters:

- **Raise a shield** — broad cover; **3 dice** if it's magic (fiction-gated).
- **A caster wards it** — spend an **action + Arcana Hearts** to cut the Cost **off the top for everyone** (the mage's sponge-tank role, beside the Leader's Heart-absorption). It costs the caster their action — a real choice between *kill the threat* and *save your friends.*
- **Take cover**, or carry **resistance** magic.

A sword or partial armor alone won't save your face. **Telegraph the AoE** ("its chest glows, brimstone gathering —") so warding is a *choice*, not a death.

## Removal & One-Shots

Two routes to take a foe out, both in Hearts:

1. **Attrition** — chip **current** Hearts to 0 (the group grind).
2. **One-shot** — overcome **current** Hearts in a single action (damage or control).

A fresh big bad has too many Hearts for any single roll to reach — so **nothing solo-removes the fresh boss**; chipping lowers the bar, the team sets up the finish, and the controller can *flip* a foe the party has worn down. Mooks fall in one; bosses are a siege. OSR one-shots survive — but only against the genuinely weak.

## Knacks — Deed-Earned

A **Knack** is a signature move recorded as a **footnote on a Trait or Skill** — not a bought advancement.

- Earned by **doing the deed**: attempt the extraordinary and land it with **Sweet.** Write it down. **No XP, doesn't count toward Alignment** — you paid in play.
- A Knack *is* a standing **Feat** ("you've done this before") → Advantage on the repeat; trivial uses free.
- Using it still runs through *Attempting the Extraordinary* (pay Hearts, capped by Alignment). It makes the deed **yours and surer**, never free-and-automatic.

**Gift Traits** (powers from death/rebirth or invocation — Lifemaster, Empathophage) are simply Traits carrying such a footnote. A footnote may **sense, read, or reveal** for free; if it would **compel**, that's no longer a sense — it routes through Influence or the spell rules.

## Influence — the Social Fix

There is **no Persuade roll**, because there is no roll for *whether* the world bends — that's Step 1.

- *"I roll to make the jailer free the prisoners."* → Against his nature/duty. **Step 1 fails it.** No roll exists.
- *"I bribe him to look away."* → Feasible. **It works** (Cost = the gold, or a Spicy twist). His mood may earn Advantage on *how well* it goes.
- *"We turn the cultists on each other."* → A *world* outcome — first manufacture the wedge in the fiction, *then* the world tips.

A creature's disposition is a **world fact** (the Guide may roll it). Character skill **amplifies a success the fiction already granted; it never manufactures an impossible one.** Social Costs are usually **coin, favor, time, or reputation** — and you can *haggle* them.

## Open Tuning Dials

- **Alignment as flat add to Hero rolls** — assumed. It grows damage/effect and caps spell scope + Extraordinary. Confirm the rate feels right.
- **Per-round venom magnitude** — scale to the threat; 8/round is apex-lethal.
- **Knack trigger on 3-die rolls** — Double Sweet is easier on bonded rolls; gate Knack-learning to a *risky/maxed* attempt if it's too generous.
- **Monster Hearts** — flat-Pow + free defense drops mooks fast; accept "mooks die in a blow" or bump pack Hearts.
- **Naming** — rename this file / fold into a clean v1.5 once playtested; reconcile with the older "Core Resolution (DRAFT)."
