# Wåndyr Level-Up!

**Optional Rules for Character Advancement**

Every adventurer starts somewhere—often a tavern on the borderlands with nothing but a rusty dagger and a dream. But with time, these wanderers grow into their legends. These optional rules for Wåndyr allow characters to level up individual Traits, Skills, and Sigils, creating more nuanced character advancement while preserving the game's elegant simplicity.

## Adding Levels to Characters

Under these optional rules, Traits, Skills, and Sigils can have levels from 1 to 10+, representing growing expertise:

- **Level 1:** Basic competence (default for new Traits)
- **Level 2-3:** Growing proficiency
- **Level 4-5:** Exceptional skill
- **Level 6-7:** Masterful ability
- **Level 8-9:** Heroic expertise
- **Level 10+:** Legendary mastery

New Traits, Skills, and Sigils still start at level 1, and characters can advance them through XP expenditure or significant accomplishments during play.

## A Simple Advancement System

This system uses a straightforward approach to character development:

1. **Adding New Abilities:** To add a new Trait, Skill, or Sigil, spend XP equal to your total number of Traits, Skills, and Sigils +1.

2. **Leveling Up:** To increase the level of an existing Trait, Skill, or Sigil, spend XP equal to the target level.

Thus, there is a balance between breadth and depth.

### Example

If your character has 4 total Traits, Skills, and Sigils combined:

- Adding any new Trait, Skill, or Sigil would cost 5 XP
- Raising an existing Trait from level 1 to level 2 would cost 2 XP
- Raising an existing Trait from level 2 to level 3 would cost 3 XP

## Using Trait Levels in Play

When a player rolls for Insight and the appropriate Trait, Skill, or Sigil has a level higher than the challenge/monster level, they can declare:

> "I'm using my [trait/skill/sigil] level [X]!"

When they do this, the Oracle target number is reduced (for example from 8 to 7, or from 10 to 9, etc.) similar to how the target is increased when the average party level is below the monster level.

### Example of Play

**Guide:** "A level 1 skeleton emerges from the crypt, its hollow eyes fixed on you. Roll for Insight."

*Player rolls a 4: Sigil*

**Player:** "I'm calling on my 'Purifying Blade' sigil, level 2!"

**Guide:** "Since your sigil level is higher than the vampire's level, you get an advantage. Roll the Oracle with a target of 7 instead of 8."

## Character Development in Play

Let's see how a character might develop over several adventures:

### Starting Character: Rook

- **Traits:** agile, cunning
- **Skills:** knife, lockpicks
- **Sigils:** none
- **Total:** 4 traits/skills/sigils
- **XP Spent:** 0 XP

### Session 5: Rook

After several adventures, Rook gains 12 XP and decides to invest as follows:

- New "shadowy" trait: 5 XP (cost of 5th trait/skill/sigil)
- Raise knife to level 2: 2 XP
- Raise knife to level 3: 3 XP
- New "Ghost of Ravencliff" sigil: 6 XP (cost of 6th trait/skill/sigil)
- **Total XP spent:** 21

## Conclusion

These optional rules add depth to character progression while preserving Wåndyr's focus on exploration and storytelling. The system rewards both specialization and diversification, allowing characters to develop naturally through their adventures.

The elegantly simple advancement system creates interesting decisions without complex bookkeeping:

- Early characters can easily acquire a variety of abilities
- As character development progresses, the increasing cost of new acquisitions naturally encourages specialization
- Players enjoy the satisfaction of calling out their higher-level traits during crucial moments

Whether your band of adventurers are hardened veterans or fresh-faced youths, these rules help chart their growth from anonymous wanderers to heroes of myth and legend whose names will echo through the ages.

## Alternatives / Variants

1. Count Traits, Skills, and Sigils separately for advancement
2. True Names such as Magic schools are a fourth kind of ability. They cost more (5-10x) to gain and level up.
