# Character Advancement

You may claim an advancement when you roll double Sweet (double 6).

**Examples:**

- Kragg cuts an orc in half with his ax. "My ax is called Halvsies."
- Lute casts *bewitch*. "I learn the spell *bewitch*."
- Jesper dodges a troll slap and stabs it in the heart. "I will call my dagger Trollbane."
- Aorfreder dispatches a spider with his longsword. "I take *longsword* skill."

The advancement must be tied to the roll — what you just did, used, or discovered. Any advancement type is available — Trait, Skill, Byname, Song, Recipe, Known Spell, Arcana, Relic, or Knack — subject to the same prerequisites as the core rules (e.g. a spell must have been cast before it can be learned; an item must have served you in a significant deed before earning a Byname; a personal Byname must be earned, not chosen).

**Prerequisite**: The Guide must have called for the roll. *The Guide only calls for rolls when the outcome is uncertain and matters — this prevents pre-rolling.*

## Fairness

*Characters should advance at a similar pace, with novices catching up faster than veterans.*

If you have already gained an advancement this session and roll double Sweet again, you may not keep it. Nominate a character with fewer total advancements than you — their player chooses what their character gains. ("You saved my life back there — what did you learn?")

Your total advancements are counted across all time, including your starting Traits and Skills.

### Option: Defer to Camp

If your table prefers to keep the momentum going rather than pause mid-session, bank the advancement instead of assigning it immediately. In camp, your table distributes all banked advancements — each goes to a character with fewer total advancements than yours, and that character's player decides what they gain.

## Game Design

This is a full replacement for the Wandyr XP system.

### Player agency drives the pace

Your engagement sets the pace. The more you take risks, push hard, and roll dice on things that matter — the more chances at double Sweet. Advancement is a direct consequence of action at the table, not a reward the GM dispenses at session end.

### No GM judgment required

The XP system asks the GM to value deeds — how many points for this rumor, does this qualify? This can create friction between the Guide and players when they don't agree.

### Advancement is tied to this moment in the game fiction

Because it happens *during* a significant roll, the advancement feels earned. You named the sword because you just did something worthy of naming it. You learned the spell because you just cast it under pressure. The narrative and the mechanics arrive together.

### Fits the Oracle philosophy

Wåndyr already uses the Oracle to generate emergent narrative rather than following a prepared story. This rule extends that philosophy to advancement — growth emerges from play, not from accounting done afterward.

### Advancements must be narratively grounded

With XP, nothing stops a player from spending points to learn a magic school they've never encountered, or a language no one in the party has heard. The tie-to-the-roll rule closes this — you can only claim what the fiction just gave you reason to know.

### Attendance and engagement are self-balancing

XP per session disadvantages players who miss time or have quieter nights. Double Sweet ties advancement to individual moments of action, so everyone advances in proportion to their actual engagement at the table.

## Potential Issues

### May interrupt active play

With XP, advancement happens entirely in camp — the accounting is offscreen and never touches the flow of a session. This rule fires in the middle of a scene, at the exact moment of dramatic tension. Rolling double Sweet during a fight, a chase, or a tense negotiation could turn into a pause: "wait, what do I learn? does this qualify? okay, I nominate Jesper — what do you want?"

Simple claims are fast ("my axe is called Halvsies" takes three seconds), but the fairness rule adds steps — nominating a character, their player deciding — and prerequisite questions can stall things further. The more novel the advancement (a Knack, a Relic, an unusual Trait), the more discussion it tends to generate.

The *Defer to Camp* option directly addresses this: bank it, keep moving, sort it out later. Tables that prioritize momentum should default to that variant.
