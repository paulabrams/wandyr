# Terrain Tables

## Instructions

**Tier 1: Climate Zone** is determined by the map or setting, not rolled. It corresponds to latitude—polar regions near the poles, equatorial near the equator.

**Tier 2: Landform** is rolled (d6) when entering a hex with a new landform. Use the table matching the hex's climate zone.

**Tier 3: Transition** is rolled (d6) when entering a new hex. It determines passage difficulty and whether the landform changes.

**Tier 4: Condition** is a GM tool. Roll (d6) when circumstances suggest environmental complications—after a storm, during a drought, when something feels off.

**Tier 5: Point of Interest** is a GM tool. Roll (d6) when you want to seed a hex with something discoverable, or when players are actively searching.

### Example

Climate zone is Temperate (known from map). Party enters a new hex from a forest.

- **Tier 3 (Transition):** 5 → Hard passage to new landform
- **Tier 2 (Landform):** 4 → Marsh or fen

*The forest gives way to boggy lowland—difficult going through mud and standing water.*

The GM decides recent weather warrants a Condition roll:

- **Tier 4 (Condition):** 5 → Storm-damaged

*Worse still, a recent storm has left fallen branches and flooded trails throughout.*

## Tier 1: Climate Zone (by Latitude)

1. Polar
2. Boreal
3. Temperate
4. Subtropical
5. Tropical
6. Equatorial

## Tier 2: Landform (d6 per Climate Zone)

### Polar

1. Tundra
2. Ice field or glacier
3. Frozen sea or lake
4. Snow plain
5. Permafrost bog
6. Rocky barrens

### Boreal

1. Taiga (conifer forest)
2. Muskeg or bog
3. Frozen lake or river
4. Open tundra
5. Rocky hills
6. River valley

### Temperate

1. Deciduous forest
2. Grassland or meadow
3. Rolling hills
4. Marsh or fen
5. River valley
6. Mixed woodland

### Subtropical

1. Scrubland or chaparral
2. Dry steppe
3. Oak woodland
4. River valley
5. Rugged hills
6. Seasonal wetland

### Tropical

1. Jungle
2. Savannah
3. Swamp or mangrove
4. River delta
5. Volcanic terrain
6. Monsoon forest

### Equatorial

1. Dense rainforest
2. Flooded forest
3. River system
4. Swamp
5. Cloud forest
6. Coastal lagoon

## Tier 3: Transition

1. Easy passage
2. Hard passage
3. Impassable barrier
4. Easy passage to new landform (roll Landform)
5. Hard passage to new landform (roll Landform)
6. Impassable barrier to new landform (roll Landform)

## Tier 4: Condition

1. Flooded or waterlogged
2. Recently burned
3. Overgrown or choked
4. Drought-stricken
5. Storm-damaged
6. Unnaturally still

## Tier 5: Point of Interest

1. Ruins or old structure
2. Campsite (old or recent)
3. Trail or crossroads
4. Water source
5. Natural shelter
6. Signs of habitation
