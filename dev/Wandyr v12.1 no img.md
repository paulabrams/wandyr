# Welcome to Wåndyr\! {#welcome-to-wåndyr!}

**Wåndyr** is an adventure game about a band of adventurers wandering through a pulp fantasy world. The focus is on exploration, following rumors, discovering places, treasure hunting, camping, singing, storytelling, cleverly overcoming impossible odds, naming weapons, and coming back alive with tales to tell. In the spirit of The Hobbit, Wåndyr celebrates the journey of going there and back again.

# Starting a Game {#starting-a-game}

Get together with friends. One is the Guide, the others are players.

#### 1 \- Starting Location

The Guide describes the starting location for the adventure, such as a medieval tavern in a town on the borderlands. The Guide can place this into a larger fantasy world, or let that be a mystery for now.

#### 2 \- Create Characters

Create three characters, each with an alias, 2 Traits and 2 Skills:

**Syr Tiriel**: pious, knight | poleax, armor  
**Lindon**: sharp-eyed, scout | longbow, ax  
**Heliön**: strong, hale | mace, shield  
**Memneth**: fiery, energetic | elementale, staff  
**Bel**: friendly, bard | Canting, lute

![Character creation illustration][image4]

#### 3 \- Select a Rumor

The Guide lists three rumors that hint about troublesome events or mysterious places to explore. Choose one rumor to follow now.

#### 4 \- Muster a Character

Roll 2d6 Hearts for each of your three characters to see how they are feeling today, then choose one to "Muster" (play). From now on, you play as your mustered character.  

#### 5 \- Pack for the Journey

The Guide will provide you with a few "hand me down" items such as weapons, armor, spellbooks, etc. The Guide may also provide  travel supplies (e.g. food, water, cloak). 

#### 6 \- Embark

The Guide will narrate the start of your journey and provide a landmark or general directions so the path forward is clear. You play your mustered character; the Guide handles everything else.

## Characters {#characters}

Each character starts with an alias, two Traits and two Skills. The Traits are how you play the character, and the Skills are what the character knows how to do.

For example, "Rook: agile cunning | knife, (lockpicks)" goes by the alias "Rook", should be played as “agile and cunning”, and knows how to use a knife and (secretly) lockpicks.

### Traits

A Trait is a keyword that describes how your character is played. *This is perhaps more important than it sounds*. This is not about what your character knows or can do, but more about how they behave. For example, a character with the "bard" Trait *acts* like a musician or skald, while Skills determine what they know how to do. You can think of these as "soft skills" that complement their "hard” Skills.

**Guidelines:** Each Trait must be a single word, or common compound word (e.g. "rat-catcher", "horse-thief"). Traits can describe physical appearance, personality, quirks, habits, customs, idioms, approach to solving problems, etc. Traits can refer to a culture, homeland, or ancestry (human, elf, dwarf, etc.) or whatever helps you paint a picture. Good Traits make the character memorable and fun to play.

![][image6]

### Skills

A Skill is something the character has learned, either an item proficiency or a knowledge. Examples: dagger, longsword, bow, lockpicks, shield, armor, type of animal, spellbook, wand, arcane staff, harp, bushcraft, etc. You can think of these as "hard skills", or technical knowledge. Skills are not about personality or behavior, but about what the character knows how to do.

**Guidelines:** Each Skill must be a single word, or common compound word corresponding to a type of item or knowledge (e.g. "greatsword", "herbal medicine").

**Specialized Skills:** Using specialized items or knowledge requires a specialized or rare Skill, such as "greatsword", "herbal medicine", or a magic school.

## The Muster {#the-muster}

*Ok boys, check your kit, check your weapons, it's today. You get a pint and a chaser, no more. Let's go*

### Hearts

**Roll 2d6 Hearts** for each of your three characters to see how they feel today. Choose one character to Muster (play) today. 

### Equipment

You have **10 Item Slots** for readily equipped gear such as armor, weapons, light, and magic items.   
Most items use one slot; larger items use multiple slots. 

#### Baggage

Anything beyond your 10 Item Slots is "Baggage", which limits movement and may prevent safely swimming, climbing, leaping, etc.

**Hands:** You can carry items in your hands beyond your 10 Item Slots. For example, you could carry a polearm, throwing ax, or torch. *You’ll have to drop them to quickly free up your hands.*

**On Your Body:** You can carry items hands free by wearing a rucksack, haversack, satchel, etc. Dropping Baggage takes 1 Round (10 seconds), but accessing items inside takes 1 Turn (10 minutes).

![Camping illustration][image7]

##### Item Slot Examples

**Small Items:** coin pouch 1, water skin 1, lockpicks 1  
**Weapons:** dagger 1, sword 2, longsword 3, greatsword 4, halberd 4, pike 5  
**Armor:** gauntlets 1, helm 1, gambeson 2, coat of plates 3, full brigandine 4  
**Shields:** buckler 1, heater 2, round shield 3, tower shield 4  
**Bows:** arrow quiver 1, bow 2, crossbow 3, arbalest 4  
**Musical Instrument:** voice 1, pipe 1, lute 2, guitar 3, drum 4  
**Clothing:** shirt 1, pants 1, boots 1, cloak 1, hat 1, ring 1, formal attire 2, cold gear 3  
**Light:** torch 1, oil flask 1, lantern 1, tinderbox 1, firewood 3  
**Rations:** 1 slot per day  
**Animals:** cat 1, hawk 1, dog 2, war dog 3, horse 4

# Playing the Game {#playing-the-game}

The game mostly consists of a conversation around the table. The Guide describes the world and people in it other than your characters. 

You can have your character attempt anything you want. The Guide decides what happens, or consults the Oracle.

## The Oracle {#the-oracle}

The Oracle answers any yes/no question. Roll 2d6: on 8 or higher the answer is “Yes”, otherwise "No".

**Easy/Hard:** The Oracle number won’t always be 8; the Guide will adjust it based on the situation. The range is 6-12: easy (6), normal (8), hard (10), or extreme (12). 

### Sweet and Spicy

If any die rolls a 6 or a 1, add a twist\! 6s are Sweet (good surprises) and 1s are Spicy (not-so-good surprises). Doubles are stronger.

*Example: An archer snipes a sentry. On "sweet" the uniform is spotless; on "spicy" another guard notices.*

The Guide can interpret the twist or allow you to provide your own interpretation. Unless the twist is straightforward and minor, ask the Oracle if it happens. *You must ante Hearts to attempt any kind of superhuman/magical stunt.*

### ![Oracle illustration][image8]Who Asks?

Your character can try anything\! When you say your character attempts a direct action, then ask the Oracle it if happens:

- “I haggle for a better price.”  
- "I throw my spear"  
- "I parry with my shield\!"

When you ask a question about something less direct, such as a fact yet unknown, the Guide asks the Oracle:

- "Is this town on a waterway?"  
- "Is there a trail in the woods?"  
- "Do they seem friendly?"

**Tip:** Phrase the question from a character perspective, where they hope for “Yes”. For example, “Do we find shelter?” is better than “Does the storm catch us?” so that a high roll (e.g. 5+6) results in “yes”.

### Insight

Have someone at the table roll d6 for “Insight” each Round or Turn. This provides you with an opportunity to claim Advantage by matching the current Insight criteria. 

Advantage lets you roll 3d6 and keep the two best, but never remove 1’s.

##### 1 \- TRAIT

You have Advantage if your approach matches your Traits.

##### 2 \- SKILL

You have Advantage if you use a helpful Skill.

##### 3 \- ITEM

You have Advantage if you use a helpful item. Special items require a specialized Skill (e.g. longbow, lockpicks, greatsword, spellbooks, wand, etc.).

##### 4 \- NAME

You have Advantage if you use a relevant Name (named item, byname, or song) you possess.

##### 5 \- MORALE

You have Advantage if your party has at least 1 Morale Heart.

##### 6 \- EFFORT

You have Advantage if you apply more effort. This is not possible with Baggage, stealth/subtlety, climbing, swimming, wrestling, or making something look easy.

### Disadvantage

A Disadvantage may apply for being unarmed, encumbered, poor position/visibility, firing a bow into or out of melee, etc. Disadvantage cancels Advantage.

### Deeper Questions

If you need more than yes/no, the Oracle can play "20 questions". The Guide should also use random tables from OSR or those in the **Wåndyr World Oracle**. 

*Guide Tip: Ask an AI to generate a table, then proof it, and then have a player roll on it.*

## Morale {#morale}

*Your character Hearts keep your character wandering, but Morale keeps your party together.*

### Morale Hearts

Morale Hearts are a shared party resource; anyone in the party can use Morale Hearts in place of their own character Hearts.

Your party gains Morale Hearts at Muster for Comforts, and can replenish them with Music & Pets.

### Comforts

*The comforts of home are cheerful companions. A wheel of cheese, a crusty bread, mead, wine, or a good smoke can make even the most dismal dungeon feel fine.*

Comforts are high-end provisions such as fine wine, spices, or fresh game. You can purchase them in town (about 10gp each) or hunt/forage in the wild. Some Comforts might only be rare gifts, such as *elven waybread.*

Each Comfort shared during Muster is worth 1 Morale Heart, up to a limit of 1 Morale Heart per player. *You can increase the limit by 1 if you have a relevant Trait/Skill: chef, cook, cooking, herbalism, etc.*

### 

### ![Time illustration][image9]Music

*Music is important because it makes everything better.*

**Performance:** You can play music to restore Morale Hearts once per Muster. The performance takes about an hour and restores 1 Morale Heart per character who participates; they will each need a musical instrument and Skill.

**Bards:** If you keep a musical instrument equipped in Item Slots continuously from Muster, you are a “bard”. You may restore 1 Morale Heart as an Action, limited to the instrument Item Slots per Muster. You can alternatively use a music Action to influence NPCs. 

### Pets

If you have a pet, they can replenish 1 Morale Heart per Item Slot the pet occupies. (Takes 1 Turn.)

## Camp {#camp}

The game starts with Muster and ends or pauses in Camp. When you decide to stop and rest, you must choose whether to make a temporary camp or return to a prior camp. The game is then paused until the next Muster. 

#### Camp Followers

Anyone not in the Muster can follow along behind to set up the next camp. You can generally fast-travel between your camps.

#### Home Base

At some point you may decide to establish a permanent camp or “home base”, such as a cave, frontier tower, tavern, or hideout. This gives you a secure place to store loot. You can have multiple bases, making fast-travel even more useful.

## Equipment {#equipment}

Wåndyr assumes a traditional fantasy economy using gold pieces (gp), where quaint medieval items are available for sale in towns.

#### Equipment Price Guide

**Common items** cost 1gp per item slot. Examples: clothing, rations, tools, etc.  
**Armaments** cost 5gp per item slot. Examples: weapons, armor, shields, arrows.  
**Niche items** cost 10gp per item slot. Examples: books, music instruments, lockpicks, and unusual munitions.  
**Masterwork** **items** are \+1 and cost an extra 100gp. Add weapon plus to damage and armor plus to Iron Hearts. Grandmasterwork are \+2, 1,000gp, and rare.  
**Elevated items** cost an extra 10gp for “good”, 100gp “rich”, 1,000gp for “noble”, and so on.  
**Magic items** have a magical feature. Elven boots silence footsteps, a hero's belt bestows strength, and a magic shield is more protective. You can usually discover its enchantment through use, especially with Skill in the item. Magic items are rarely sold.

## Experience {#experience}

*New characters are unproven, untested novices with no prior experience, or heroic deeds. All this must develop during the game.*

You will earn 1-6 Experience Points (XP) after following your selected rumor to its full conclusion and returning to camp.

You can also earn small spot bonuses of 1 XP for deeds important in Wandyr: discovering an important new location or magical feature, mapping a new region, experimenting with magic, or rescuing captives. *Slaying monsters and looting treasure never directly earns XP.*

### Traits and Skills

You can spend XP in camp. You start with two Traits and two Skills. Your 3rd Trait is 3 XP, 4th is 4 XP, etc. Your 3rd Skill is 3 XP, 4th is 4 XP, etc.

### Advancements

There are other ways to spend XP:

- Item Names  
- Bynames  
- Songs  
- Spells  
- Arcana  
- Relics

##### Item Names

Once an item has served you well in a significant deed, you can give it a Name. For example, after narrowly slaying a horrid troll you might name your sword “Trollbane”. Naming your first item is 1 XP. Naming your 2nd is 2 XP, 3rd is 3 XP, etc.  
*An artisan can inscribe the Name and add decorations (inlays, etchings, jewels, gold leaf, gold pommel, etc.).*

##### Bynames

A “byname” is a personal epithet, noble title, or True Name gained through epic deeds. For example, after slaying a dragon, you might earn "Dragonslayer”, “the Bold”, or "Hero of Ravenwald". Your first byname is 1 XP, 2nd is 2 XP, 3rd is 3 XP, etc. *However, bynames are usually to you rather than something you pick for yourself.*

##### Spells

If you are a magic-user you might start with a spellbook, but no **known spells**. Learning a 1st level spell is 1d6 XP. Your 2nd level is that spell 2 XP, 3rd is 3 XP, etc. This is separate for each spell. To learn a spell you must first cast it successfully *with Sweet*. Once you learn a spell at 1st level, you can cast it without the book.

##### Arcana

You can transfer a known spell from a spellbook to an item, creating an Arcana. Your first Arcana costs 1 XP,  2nd is 2 XP, 3rd is 3 XP, etc.

##### Relics

A Relic is a conduit to an otherworldly being. Creating your first Relic is 1 XP, 2nd is 2 XP, 3rd is 3 XP, etc.

### Character Level

Your “Character Level” is your number of Traits. The Guide uses character Level for setting the Oracle target. If the characters have a “party leader” the Guide can use their Level instead.

Each category list has its own level. The Guide might use the number of Skills when hard knowledge is more appropriate, and magic-users have a different level for each magic school based on their number of spells known.

### Sandbox Play {#sandbox-play}

The Guide describes the world and serves to answer your questions. The Guide largely consults the Oracle.

The local area remains wide open and undefined, even if some aspects of the wider world are known. This "sandbox" style allows everyone at the table to discover the unexpected together.

The Guide doesn't create a story beforehand. Instead, the Guide uses dice rolls and random tables to determine what happens. Each new discovery is a surprise to everyone and adds to the growing lore of your campaign world. 

### Time

To add a sense of urgency, the Guide tracks 10-minute "Turns" using a "Turn Clock". This can be a glass or metal bowl in the middle of the table. The Guide loudly tosses a die into the clock each time you do something time-consuming. When the bowl is full with all six dice, an hour has passed; the Guide empties it and rolls the dice. Any 1's indicate a spicy complication such as a wandering monster or guard. The Guide also rolls the Turn Clock when you do something reckless.

#### Combat Rounds

In combat, time is measured in 10-second "Rounds". This is just enough time for each character to move and take one action. The Turn Clock is usually paused in combat.

# Mågįc {#mågįc}

Wåndyr is full of magic, and much of the game is about exploring and meddling with magical secrets.

All magic in Wåndyr involves *True Names*, either written or spoken as words of power. A True Name can provide a magic enhancement to an item, a sorcery to bind a spirit into a “sentient” item, a sigil for a magic-user spell, and much more.

Magic is classified by “magic school”. Words are magical, and magic is formed by magic words, runes, glyphs, arcane script, etc.

## Magic-Users {#magic-users}

If you have a Skill in a Magic School, you are a "magic-user" literate in the secret magic language of that school. The major schools are:

1. Vitae (Life)  
2. Elementale (Elemental)  
3. Thaumaturgy (Control)  
4. Illusione (Illusion)  
5. Umbrakallo (Shadow Gates)  
6. Necromantia (Death)  
7. Canting (Thievery)

All magic items and magical objects bear mystical inscriptions: sharp dwarven runes, flowing elven script, or shifting arcane glyphs, only decipherable by *magic-users*.

![Magic items illustration][image10]

### Spell Casting

If you are a magic-user, you can use an action to cast a spell. You need to state which spell you are casting and declare what you want your spell to do. This is important because in Wåndyr spells have simple open-ended names that enable you to make your magic unique.

Next, you must spend 2 Hearts and then ask the Oracle if your spell functions as you wished. The Guide sets the Oracle target number based on the situation and what you said you are trying to do. For example, if you are attempting something new or more powerful than ever before, the Oracle will probably be “hard” (+2).

The Guide describes the outcome of the spellcasting based on your roll. On “yes” the spell will largely do what you wanted. On “no” the spell does NOT do what you wanted, with variable results. A roll with Sweet and/or Spicy will have a surprising twist..

**Prerequisites:** Casting a spell requires a Spell written in a Spellbook. You must say the magic words loudly and precisely. You must use both your hands to weave the spell. Later, once you have learned the spell (i.e. you are casting a “known spell”) you no longer need the book and can use one hand.

##### Spell Casting Time

Spellcasting takes 1 Turn (10 minutes). Fast-casting a spell in combat takes 1 Round (10 seconds), but the effects take place at the very end of the Round.

##### Spell Duration

You can attempt a spell with almost any duration desired; just take this into account with your *ante* of Hearts. You could cast a "blinding flash" spell with an instantaneous duration, or a "shield" spell to last for the current combat, or even the Muster (day).

### Learning Spells

Once you have cast a spell with Sweet, you are eligible to learn the spell. (See [Experience](#experience).)

You no longer need a spellbook to cast a known spell, and only need one free hand instead of both. You can also ante Hearts up to your caster level to boost the spell.

### Caster Level

You have a separate caster level in each magic school, which is simply the number of spells you know in that school. This determines your level of magic literacy and how many Hearts you can spend to boost a spell. For example, if you know 3 Umbrakalla spells, your Umbrakalla caster level is 3, so you can spend up to 3 Hearts when casting an Umbrakalla spell.

*When using a spellbook, treat your caster level as 1 level higher.*

### Magic Components

A Magic Component reduces this cost by 1 Heart per item slot consumed. *Magic components can be found in strange magical places.*

## Arcana {#arcana}

An Arcana is a drippy spellbook. They can be any kind of item (not just books) as long as they have heavy magic-user "drip". The spells are written in stylized words of power rather than overly wordy novellas. Even tattoos are possible. To other people they appear like some kind of “wizard fashion statement”.

#### Using Arcana

Arcana are used as spellbooks. They are favored by travelling magic-users, since they are generally more portable and durable than spellbooks.

#### Creating Arcana

You can create an Arcana by transferring a *known* *spell* from a spellbook to an item. (This costs XP, so you will need to keep a list of Arcana spells.)

The *power words* from the spell vanish from the book and appear on the item. The spellbook can no longer be used to cast that spell. In theory this can be reversed to restore the book, if all goes well.

## Sentient Items {#sentient-items}

Sentient items contain a bound soul of a magic-user, elemental, *d'jinn, ifr'it, kha'din*, dragon, eldar, deep one, or other entity. Bound spirits are notoriously capricious, jealous, and unhinged from confinement, their original dreams and ambitions maybe twisted by madness.

Sentient items are temperamental; when you first attempt to use one, ask the Oracle if the item \*accepts\* you. On Sweet or Spicy, the item also talks to you\!

If you carry a sentient item, your character grows in *alignment* and may discover deeper mysteries. You might take a Trait such as "Wielder of Duskbringer" to reflect this bond.  It's petty, but they *hate* anyone who offends their fragile egos, such as carrying them after being rejected, refusing to follow their agenda, abandoning, or "cheating" on them with a rival item.

Powers in a sentient item can vary widely, ranging from a magic item with guessable powers to Arcana or even Relics of otherworldly powers.

## Invocations {#invocations}

You can make an Invocation to an otherworldly being by loudly and formally calling their True Name *thrice* and making a request.

#### Lore

Look for True Names in mythic places, written on runestones, tombs, or magic amulets. Randomly generate each new name; beings can have many names. You might want to ask the Oracle “have I heard this *Name* before?”, “is this being aligned with Law?”, etc. All this adds to the known Lore of the *being*.

#### Making a Request

An Invocation is a request for a boon or curse. This should include a valuable offering such as Hearts or better. Ask the Oracle if the being responds to your request. On Sweet they *like* the request, on Spicy they *dislike* it.

#### An Answer?

The Guide follows existing Lore to interpret the answer, and will likely ask the Oracle what the being does (if anything) and when, how, etc. The Guide will consider the personality of the being and the alignment of your character. 

*The workings of otherworldly beings are often mysterious. They may interpret your request from a different perspective, and may have a different sense of time.*  
![][image13]

#### Alignment

Making an Invocation changes your character. The being will choose a Trait that brings you into alignment with *them*. You must buy this Trait; delay or refusal may draw their ire. Other demands are possible.

#### Relics

A Relic is a sacred object that serves as conduit to the otherworldly being. Relics act much like *Sentient Items*, inscrutable and opinionated.

# Fighting {#fighting}

In each Round, you can Move and Attack (or take another action).

### Move

You can stand up OR move 40 feet (20 feet with Baggage). Each impediment (e.g. readying weapons, bad terrain, darkness) cuts this in half.

### Attack

Ask the Oracle if you "hit"; this deals d6 Hearts. More lethal weapons may have a "plus" bonus, e.g. "greatsword \+1". 

### Defend

When you are attacked or otherwise endangered, ask the Oracle if you avoid getting "hit". If you are hit, the Guide rolls Hearts lost (often d6). Reduce by 1 Heart if you melee attack or parry the foe this round.

Armor and shields give 1 "iron" Heart per item slot, usable only against weapons. More protective armor may have a "plus" bonus, such as "shield \+1".

### Initiative

The Guide determines the order in which players and enemies act based on the situation. The front of the party is usually called on before the rear. 

When the action starts too far away for melee, only range attacks happen. Characters far out of frame may need to move before acting.

Melee attacks go first up close or in low-vis. Melee is usually simultaneous, but, when approaching from a distance the weapon with the better reach (by item slots) has the "right of way" to attack first. In close quarters combat (under 10 feet of space per person) the smaller weapon has the “right of way” to attack first. Attackers are open to immediate counterattacks.

Non-weapon actions such as spells happen at the end of the round.

## Monsters {#monsters}

Each monster (or trap, etc.) has a **Level** comparable to **Character Level**. The Guide sets the Oracle to “hard” if this is higher than Character Level; or “easy” if lower. Consider using the party leader's level when working together.

**Level 0:** giant rat, vermin, goblin  
**Level 1:** orc, skeleton, deathsquito  
**Level 2:** zombie, ghoul, giant ant, slime  
**Level 3:** wight, harpy, giant spider  
**Level 4:** werewolf, ogre, huge slime  
**Level 5:** mummy, minotaur, owlbeast  
**Level 6:** troll, wyvern, spectre, manticore  
**Level 7:** griffon, djinni, vampire  
**Level 9:** chimera, giant  
**Level 10:** golem, dragon

For more detail, the Guide can use OSR monsters or wing it as follows.  
**Pack/tribal monsters** have d6 Hearts and deal d6 damage per hit; leaders have double.  
**A solitary “apex predator” monster** has 2d6 or 3d6 Hearts per HD, multiple attacks, plus a deadly attack of 2d6 or 3d6, or d6 Hearts per Round for HD Turns (for paralysis, poison, petrification, etc).

## Death {#death}

Characters and monsters die at 0 Hearts. If you reach 0 Hearts, choose whether you die "offscreen" or "onscreen".

**Offscreen:** We cut away as your character drops; bring in another character next round.

Offscreen deaths are uncertain. At any future Muster, you may let the character pass on, or make an Invocation to return. If the Invocation roll is **Double Spicy** (double 1s), you are dead with no uncertainty.

**Onscreen:** Describe one final heroic action on the next round; ask the Oracle if the action succeeds and then go Offscreen. *Heroic feats require Sweet.* *Spicy indicates immediate, final death.*
