# Damage Dice

## Base Damage

In Wandyr, most attacks deal d6 damage. This keeps things simple and fast. For example, a sword attack deals d6 damage, a bow attack deals d6 damage, a bite attack deals d6 damage, etc.

## Extra Damage

Weapons can have a plus, such as +1 or +2, which increases damage by the plus. For example, a sword +1 deals d6+1 damage, a sword +2 deals d6+2 damage. Magical weapons can have higher pluses, such as +3 or +4.

### Monsters

Tribal monsters (or human guards, etc.) typically have d6 Hearts and deal d6 damage. Their leaders may have a plus (if using a weapon) or deal 2d6 damage (if it's an alpha bite attack, etc).

"Apex predator" monsters can make multiple attacks, and also have a "deadly" attack that can deal 2d6 or 3d6 damage, or an equivalently "deadly" effect such as poison, petrification, disease, etc. The notion of being "deadly" is relative to the 2d6 Hearts characters receive as their base, meaning a character without any armor (iron hearts) or other protection can be one-shotted by them. It's usually more fun if the monster doesn't one-shot them, but the threat of it is there.

The Guide may use B/X or AD&D monsters with other damage dice, or approxiate damage using d6s.

## Damage Resistance

If the monster is resistant to damage from the weapon or damage type, it takes full effect on Sweet, and otherwise ignored. Also, on Spicy, there is a side effect: a fighter striking the ghost with his mundane sword might find it chills and the metal becomes brittle (if used to attack again before thawing it will break!).

### Alternative: Fixed Damage Reduction

If a monster is resistant to a damage type, reduce damage taken. For example, an undead might take away 1 damage from a normal weapon attack, since it has not internal organs or nervous system. Or an "iron golem" might be stacked with armor plates, reducing damage taken by 2 from all attacks.

An incorporeal monster such as a ghost might be immune to non-magical weapons, taking no damage unless the weapon is magical.

### Alternative: Damage Rolls vs Resistance

As an alternative, the Guide may allow damage rolls of 4-6 penetrate resistance, while low rolls of 1-3 have no effect. This preserves the player victory moment of a high damage roll.

## Vulnerability

If a monster is vulnerable to a damage type, it takes extra damage from that type. For example, a troll might take +1 or double damage from fire-based attacks.
