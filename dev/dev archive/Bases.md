# Bases

Bases are places where characters can rest, recover, and fast-travel between locations. They represent safe havens, home camps, or established hideouts where the party has invested time and resources. When characters travel between bases, they can fast-forward through the journey, skipping the mundane travel and arriving at their destination.

This keeps the game focused on meaningful choices and exploration rather than travel logistics, while still maintaining the weight of distance and the cost of establishing new safe havens.

## ESTABLISHING A BASE

A Base is a location where the party has:

- **Spent significant time** (at least one full Muster/Camp session)
- **Made it safe** (cleared threats, established defenses, or negotiated peace)
- **Left supplies or resources** (food, tools, equipment, or trusted contacts)
- **Marked it clearly** (the Guide should know where it is and how to describe returning)

Bases can be:

- A cleared dungeon room with barricades
- A friendly inn or tavern where you're known
- A hidden cave or campsite
- A safe house in a city
- A ship or wagon that travels with you
- A trusted NPC's home or establishment

**Starting Base:** Characters begin with one Base (their starting location, home village, or initial safe haven).

## FAST-TRAVEL

When traveling from one Base to another, characters can **fast-travel**, which means:

- **Skip the journey** - The Guide narrates the travel briefly ("You spend three days on the road, encountering nothing of note") rather than playing out every encounter
- **Arrive at destination** - Characters arrive at the destination Base, ready for the next adventure
- **No random encounters** - Fast-travel skips wandering monster checks and random events
- **Time still passes** - The journey takes the appropriate amount of time (days, weeks, etc.)

**Limitations:**

- Fast-travel only works **between established Bases**
- Characters must have **actually been to both locations** (you can't fast-travel to a place you've never visited)
- The Guide can **interrupt fast-travel** if something narratively important happens ("As you're traveling, you notice...")
- Fast-travel assumes **safe passage** - if the route is blocked or dangerous, normal travel rules apply

## FAST-FORWARD

When characters are at a Base and want to skip time (healing, crafting, training, waiting for events), they can **fast-forward**:

- **Time passes** - Days, weeks, or months pass as needed
- **Resources refresh** - Characters recover Hearts, replenish supplies, restock equipment
- **Narrative events** - The Guide can introduce time-sensitive events, rumors, or changes
- **Camp Actions** - Characters can still take Camp Actions during fast-forward (one per time period)

**Limitations:**

- Fast-forward assumes the Base remains **safe and stable**
- Characters can't fast-forward through **active danger** or ongoing threats
- The Guide can **interrupt fast-forward** with events, encounters, or complications
- Fast-forward doesn't skip **meaningful choices** - if something important happens, play it out

## BASE BENEFITS

Bases provide:

- **Safe resting place** - Characters can recover Hearts and rest without interruption
- **Supply cache** - Stored equipment, food, and resources are available
- **Home advantage** - Characters know the layout, can set traps, and have local knowledge
- **NPC connections** - Trusted contacts, allies, or hired help at the Base
- **Fast-travel hub** - Can quickly move between Bases

## BASE MAINTENANCE

Bases require some upkeep:

- **Abandoned Bases** - If a Base isn't visited for a long time (multiple sessions), it might be looted, claimed by others, or fall into disrepair
- **Threatened Bases** - If enemies know about a Base, they might attack it or lay traps
- **Upgraded Bases** - Characters can invest resources to improve Bases (better defenses, more storage, NPC guards, etc.)

The Guide should ask the Oracle occasionally: "Is the Base still safe when they return?" to inject tension and consequences.

## MULTIPLE BASES

Characters can establish multiple Bases throughout the campaign. Each Base represents a different location, resource, or connection. Fast-travel between Bases creates a network of safe havens, allowing the party to operate across larger areas without getting bogged down in travel logistics.

**Example:** A party might have:

- Base 1: Their home village
- Base 2: A cleared dungeon room in the Vault of First Light
- Base 3: A friendly inn in the nearest town
- Base 4: A hidden cave in the mountains

They can fast-travel between any of these, but must still explore normally to reach new locations.

## GM NOTES

Bases are a tool for **pacing and convenience**, not a way to skip meaningful content. Use fast-travel and fast-forward to:

- Skip boring travel when nothing interesting happens
- Move quickly between known locations
- Allow characters to recover and restock
- Maintain narrative momentum

But **don't use Bases to skip**:

- First-time exploration of new areas
- Dangerous or meaningful travel
- Time-sensitive events or deadlines
- Player choices about routes or destinations

The Guide should feel free to interrupt fast-travel or fast-forward when something narratively interesting happens. Bases exist to make the game smoother, not to eliminate all travel and time.
