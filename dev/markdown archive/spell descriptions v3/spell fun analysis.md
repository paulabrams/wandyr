# Analysis of Spells for Fun and Consistency with Wåndyr

## **Wåndyr’s core magic rules**

- **Schools and numbering.**  Wåndyr organises spells into six schools – Vitae (1.x.x), Elementalis (2.x.x), Thaumaturgy (3.x.x), Illusione (4.x.x), Umbrakalla (5.x.x) and Necromantia (6.x.x).  Each number reflects the school, the level (1‑6) and a spell within that level.  Each school is described thematically, e.g., Elementalis deals with weather and elements.
    
- **Spell casting.**  Casting a spell requires a spellbook and a free hand.  The caster must speak the power words, and the spell costs Hearts equal to its level; a magic component can reduce this cost by one.  When a spell is cast, the **Oracle** is consulted to determine whether the magic user’s intent happens, producing Sweet or Spicy outcomes.
    
- **Duration.**  Spells are either **Instant** (one action) or **Active** (two actions, lasting the scene).  Active spells end when the scene ends.  A magic‑user may later spend XP equal to the spell’s level to learn it as a **True Name**, allowing casting without a spellbook.
    

  

These rules emphasise simple resolution via the Oracle and hearts expenditure rather than fixed damage numbers or saving throws common in D&D.  Any supplementary spell list should adhere to this model.

  

## **Canting (Roguish) spells**

  

**Theme and fun:**  The Canting school introduces “flash patter” magic used by thieves and scoundrels.  It is filled with evocative street slang and focuses on enhancing thieving skills.  Spells such as **Steady Fambles** steady the caster’s hands for delicate work, **Darkmans Creeping** grants the ability to move silently through darkness, and **Sucker Punch** enables a devastating surprise attack.  Higher‑level spells (not reproduced here) offer dramatic heist‑fantasy abilities such as **Break the Ward** to unlock any lock or **The Foist’s Art** to pickpocket anything.  The use of argot makes the spells flavourful and fun to read.

  

**Consistency issues:**

1. **School numbering.**  The Canting list uses **6.x.x** codes (e.g., 6.1.1 Steady Fambles).  Wåndyr already assigns 6.x.x to Necromantia (death) spells.  To avoid confusion, the Canting school should receive a new school number (e.g., 7.x.x) or be treated as a sub‑discipline under Vitae or Thaumaturgy.
    
2. **Terminology and duration.**  Canting spells label their sustained effects as **“Sorcery”** rather than **“Active”**.  Wåndyr’s rules define only **Instant** and **Active** spells, so the sorcery term should be replaced with **Active** and clarified as requiring two actions and a scene‑long duration.
    
3. **Mechanics.**  Many descriptions mention improved accuracy or silence but do not specify Heart costs.  Wåndyr spells cost Hearts equal to their level; the Canting spells should state that casting costs Hearts accordingly (e.g., level 1 Canting spells cost 1 Heart) and rely on the Oracle to decide success rather than guaranteeing success.  Spells such as **Sucker Punch**, which deals maximum possible damage, should instead confer advantage or a Sweet outcome rather than fixed damage, because damage in Wåndyr is normally 1d6 per hit.
    
4. **Power level.**  Later Canting spells (e.g., Perfect Crime or Steal History) can rewrite events.  These effects are thematically fun but potentially campaign‑breaking.  They could be recast as legendary boons that require rare components and an Oracle check with high risk.
    

  

## **Hallowed (Divine) spells**

  

**Theme and fun:**  The Hallowed school channels nobility, wisdom and heavenly authority.  Low‑level spells such as **Authority** create an aura of leadership, **Blessing** grants divine favor for advantage and **Celestial Light** dispels darkness and reveals hidden things.  Higher levels bring potent holy abilities like **Smite Evil** (extra damage to evil creatures), **Divine Ascension** (briefly becoming a divine being) and **Word of Creation** (commanding reality itself).  These spells are flavourful and capture the fantasy of paladins or clerics.

  

**Consistency issues:**

1. **Numbering conflict.**  Hallowed spells use **5.x.x** codes (e.g., 5.1.1 Authority).  In Wåndyr, 5.x.x designates Umbrakalla (shadow) spells.  To integrate Hallowed spells, assign them a different number (e.g., 7.x.x if Canting is omitted or 8.x.x) or treat them as an expansion of Vitae (life) spells.
    
2. **Terminology.**  These spells follow the **Instant/Active** structure and are thus largely compatible with Wåndyr.  However, some descriptions reference concepts like “advantage”, “saving throws” or “hit points” familiar to D&D (e.g., **Smite Evil** deals extra damage in d6s).  Wåndyr uses the Oracle to determine success and deals damage in Hearts (1d6 per hit).  It would be better to describe the effect as granting advantage on the Oracle roll or dealing an extra Heart of damage rather than fixed d6 damage.
    
3. **Power balance.**  High‑level spells such as **Word of Creation** and **Judgment of the Gods** can alter reality or affect everyone within 100 feet, which may eclipse other magic.  In Wåndyr, such spells should require significant XP expenditure or the intervention of a deity.  The Guide may use the Oracle to determine side‑effects or costs.
    

  

## **Elementalis (Elemental) spells**

  

**Theme and fun:**  Elementalis spells expand the brief list in the Wåndyr rulebook with colourful effects.  Spells like **Control Weather** allow subtle weather shifts; **Dragon’s Breath** lets the caster exhale flame; **Earthquake** produces tremors that knock foes over; and **Element Master** enables simultaneous control over fire, water, earth and air.  Many spells include tactical applications such as creating walls, manipulating gravity, or heating metal.  These evocative powers make the elemental school exciting.

  

**Consistency issues:**

1. **Mechanical terminology.**  Some spells specify damage numbers (e.g., **Dragon’s Breath** deals 1d6 damage, **Elemental Wall** has 5 Hearts of durability).  While hearts and damage are part of Wåndyr, the use of D&D‑style saving throws or advantage is absent here, which is good.  However, damage could be handled via the Oracle (e.g., treat the 1d6 as the damage inflicted on a hit).  Clarify that the cost in Hearts is equal to spell level and that damage is adjudicated using the Oracle rather than automatic.
    
2. **Complexity.**  Many Elementalis spells have detailed mechanics (ranges, durations, weight limits).  Wåndyr generally favours rulings over rules; these details can be simplified, with the Oracle determining whether the effect succeeds and its scale.
    
3. **Power scaling.**  Level entries describe increased range or number of elements controlled.  This matches Wåndyr’s concept of increasing potency with higher levels.  However, explicit mention that additional effects require paying extra Hearts would help integrate the spells.
    

  

## **Umbrakalla (Shadow) spells**

  

**Theme and fun:**  The Umbrakalla spells expand Wåndyr’s shadow‑gate themes.  They allow mind tricks (**Befuddle**), teleportation (**Dimension Door**), and personal protection (**Cloak of Shadows**).  Spells such as **Gate Sense** detect portals and **Ice Heart** inflicts chilling damage.  Higher‑level spells (not fully quoted) include shadow melding, plane shifting and void gates.  These powers are thematically consistent with a dark, planar traveller and are fun for players seeking mobility and trickery.

  

**Consistency issues:**

1. **Numbering conflict.**  The Umbrakalla file uses the same 5.x.x codes as the core Wåndyr Umbrakalla list, so they are effectively an expansion rather than a new school.  That is acceptable if clearly marked as additional spells.
    
2. **D&D terminology.**  Some spells refer to saving throws (e.g., Ice Heart reduces speed and causes movement penalties) or specify conditions like immunity to physical attacks while scattered.  In Wåndyr, the Oracle resolves such effects; thus, descriptions should indicate that the Oracle decides whether an opponent is hindered or takes damage.
    
3. **Duration clarifications.**  The file uses **Instant** and **Active** correctly, but some spells mention “2+ actions” for Active effects.  Wåndyr states that Active spells require two actions and last for the scene.  “2+ actions” can be simplified to follow that rule.
    

  

## **Illusione (Illusion) spells**

  

**Theme and fun:**  Illusione spells offer deception, sensory manipulation and mind‑bending.  **Arcane Eye** creates a remote sensor; **Auditory Illusion** conjures any sound; **Bewitch** clouds thoughts; and **Disguise** provides instant make‑overs.  Other spells cover dream communication, duplication, emotional manipulation, false memories and invisibility.  These powers are flavourful and encourage creative play.

  

**Consistency issues:**

1. **Mechanics borrowed from other systems.**  Several spells reference advantage/disadvantage and saving throws (e.g., Bewitch causes disadvantage on the target’s next action; Blur gives attackers disadvantage).  In Wåndyr, the Oracle determines outcomes rather than using D&D mechanics, and characters do not have saving throws.  These spells should describe desired effects in narrative terms and rely on the Oracle to adjudicate whether the illusion fools targets.  For example, Bewitch might cause confusion or hesitation; the Oracle decides whether the target is affected.
    
2. **Heart costs and level scaling.**  The spells do not mention Hearts.  They should state that casting costs Hearts equal to the spell level and that the Level entry increases potency for additional Heart expenditure.
    

  

## **Vitae (Life) spells**

  

**Theme and fun:**  Vitae spells celebrate life, growth and harmony.  They include animal bonding, healing, pacification and plant communication.  For example, **Blood Healing** lets you sacrifice a Heart to restore 2 Hearts to another; **Joy Song** removes fear and uplifts allies; **Snail Knight** summons a giant snail mount with Hearts and armour; and **Sun Beam** produces sunlight that damages undead.  These are imaginative and support a nurturing play style.

  

**Consistency issues:**

1. **Damage and saves.**  Some spells specify that undead take 1d6 damage or creatures must make Wisdom saves (in other parts).  Replace these with narrative descriptions and rely on the Oracle for outcomes.  For example, Sun Beam could simply cause undead to recoil and lose Hearts as determined by the Oracle.
    
2. **Heart costs.**  The file sometimes uses Hearts as resource (e.g., Blood Healing costs your own Hearts to heal another), which fits Wåndyr.  Ensure all Vitae spells explicitly cost Hearts equal to their level when cast.
    
3. **Duration and scaling.**  The Instant/Active structure is largely followed.  Level improvements, such as a larger snail mount with more Hearts, match Wåndyr’s progression.
    

  

## **Thaumaturgy (Control) spells**

  

**Theme and fun:**  Thaumaturgy spells manipulate the physical world.  **Adhere** glues objects together, **Animate Object** makes tools obey, **Astral Prison** encases a target in crystal stasis, **Force Push** projects a wave of energy dealing 1d6 bludgeoning damage, and **Haste** accelerates movement.  These spells feel like engineering magic and offer many tactical options.

  

**Consistency issues:**

1. **D&D mechanics.**  Many spells refer to “Hit Dice,” “saving throws,” “Dex save,” or grant extra actions (e.g., Haste) that align with D&D rather than Wåndyr.  In Wåndyr, characters do not have extra actions beyond the Move/Action structure and the Oracle resolves success.  To adapt, describe the effect narratively (e.g., Haste allows the target to move twice as far and attempt a second action if the Oracle permits) and require payment of Hearts.
    
2. **Damage.**  Spells like Force Push specify 1d6 bludgeoning damage.  Because Wåndyr damage is always 1d6 per hit, this is acceptable if rephrased as dealing an extra hit on a Sweet result.  Alternatively, treat it as automatically knocking targets back and let the Oracle decide if they are hurt.
    
3. **Range and weight limits.**  The spells often include detailed numerical limits (e.g., support up to 25 pounds).  Wåndyr normally lets the Guide set reasonable boundaries; the numbers can be kept as guidelines but shouldn’t restrict creative play.
    

  

## **Necromantia (Death) spells**

  

**Theme and fun:**  Necromantia spells embrace macabre horror and control over death.  **Animate Skeleton** raises a skeleton to perform tasks; **Black Sacrament** sacrifices the caster’s hit point to empower future necromancy; **Blood Curse** hexes a target at the cost of the caster’s health; **Corpse Explosion** detonates a corpse to deal necrotic damage; and **Death Gate** opens a portal to the underworld.  These spells are dramatically fun for players who enjoy dark magic.

  

**Consistency issues:**

1. **Numbering conflict.**  Necromantia spells are correctly numbered as 6.x.x according to Wåndyr, but the Canting list also uses 6.x.x, causing overlap.  If Canting becomes a new school, Necromantia retains 6.x.x.
    
2. **Mechanics from other systems.**  The descriptions refer to Hit Dice, AC bonuses, Wisdom saving throws and fixed damage (e.g., Corpse Explosion deals 1d6 necrotic damage to all within 10 feet).  These should be converted to Wåndyr’s mechanics: the Oracle resolves whether the explosion harms nearby creatures, and the damage is measured in Hearts rather than hit points.  AC bonuses could be rephrased as extra “iron” Hearts as per Wåndyr’s armor system.
    
3. **Heart costs.**  Necromantia spells rarely state the Hearts required to cast; they should cost Hearts equal to their level.  Effects that sacrifice the caster’s life (e.g., Black Sacrament) could require additional Hearts or permanent sacrifice to balance their power.
    

  

## **Overall assessment**

- **Fun factor:**  All of the spell lists are imaginative and rich with theme.  The Canting school introduces a refreshing roguish perspective; Hallowed spells capture the feel of divine miracles; Elementalis, Illusione and Umbrakalla build on existing Wåndyr schools with vivid descriptions; Vitae spells nurture life and nature; Thaumaturgy offers inventive physics manipulation; and Necromantia spells embrace dark, necromantic fantasy.  Players who enjoy narrative flavour will appreciate the creative names, slang and vivid descriptions.
    
- **Mechanical consistency:**  Many spells were clearly written with D&D or other systems in mind.  They reference hit points, armour class, advantage/disadvantage, saving throws and predetermined damage dice.  Wåndyr’s rules emphasize a simpler system: **Hearts** are the resource spent to cast spells, damage is measured by hits dealing 1d6 Hearts, and outcomes are determined by asking the **Oracle**.  To integrate these spells into Wåndyr, the following adjustments are recommended:
    
    - **Rename durations.**  Use **Instant** and **Active** consistently; avoid new terms like “Sorcery.”
        
    - **Specify Heart costs.**  Each spell level should cost an equal number of Hearts to cast, possibly reduced by a magic component.
        
    - **Replace D&D mechanics.**  Remove references to advantage/disadvantage, AC, saving throws, Hit Dice and extra actions.  Instead, describe desired outcomes narratively and let the Oracle adjudicate.
        
    - **Clarify level scaling.**  Wåndyr spells become more powerful with higher level; this can be expressed as greater range, more targets or additional Hearts cost.
        
    - **Resolve numbering clashes.**  Assign unique school numbers to new schools like Canting or Hallowed to avoid overlapping with existing lists.
        
    

  

By making these adjustments, the creative spells provided can enrich the Wåndyr magic system while preserving its elegant and improvisational design.
