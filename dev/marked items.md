# Marked Items

Items bearing a smith's mark—a stamped insignia identifying the craftsperson or forge—are of notably superior quality. A mark is not magical; it represents exceptional materials and workmanship.

## Marked Weapons

A marked weapon bites true where a lesser blade might turn. When rolling damage with a marked weapon, reroll any 1s (keep the new result).

## Marked Armor and Shields

Marked armor and shields turn aside blows that would cut through common protection. When an attacker rolls damage against someone whose armor is fully marked, reroll any 6s (keep the new result).

A character is only considered marked if *all* worn armor and shield pieces bear a mark. A chain is only as strong as its weakest link—one unmarked piece means a blow can find the gap.

## Marked vs. Marked

When a marked weapon strikes marked armor, both cancel. Roll straight.

## Cost

Armaments cost 5 gp per item slot. Examples: weapons, armor, shields. Marked armaments bear a smith's mark of quality and cost an additional 500 gp per item. They are found only where skilled craftspersons work.

Marking a full suit of armor requires every piece to be of quality. A 5-slot harness costs 2,525 gp to fully mark (25 gp base + 2,500 gp in marks). A single marked sword costs 510 gp (10 gp base + 500 gp mark).

## Finding Marked Items

Marked items are not common goods. A player who wants marked equipment must find a skilled craftsperson—a named smith or reputable forge—and commission the work. Not every town has one. The Guide decides what is available.

## Famous Marks

Some marks carry reputations. A blade stamped with the running wolf of Passau or the crossed hammers of a legendary dwarf-hold is recognized by those who know arms and armor. The Guide is encouraged to create named smiths and forge-marks as part of the setting.

Players may ask "is the foe's sword marked?" A seasoned warrior would naturally assess an opponent's gear.

## Upgrading to a Mark

Can I have my existing weapon or armor marked? No. A mark reflects quality built into the item from the start—the choice of steel, the forging process, the tempering. You need a new item made by a skilled hand.

## Losing a Piece

If a marked armor piece is lost or destroyed, the character is no longer fully marked until it is replaced with another marked piece. This makes completing and maintaining a marked harness a meaningful commitment.

## Magical Items

Items with a "plus" (e.g., +1, +2) are always magical. Magical bonuses are something beyond craft. A magical bonus stacks with a smith's mark—a marked sword +1 rerolls 1s on damage *and* adds its magical bonus.

## Legacy Items

Items from earlier adventures that bear a non-magical +1 or +2 bonus continue to function as they always have. Going forward, all new non-magical quality is represented by marks; all plusses are magical.
