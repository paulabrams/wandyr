## **TURN CLOCK ISSUE: Players Not Noticing Mechanics**

The turn clock isn't creating the intended time pressure if players aren't observing it. Need to either:

1. **Make it more visible/obvious** - physical prop, verbal announcements
2. **Replace with different mechanic** - if it's not working, iterate on something that does
3. **Simplify the system** - maybe just announce "time passes" more directly

**CURRENT SYSTEM**: Metal bowl + glass beads (loud when adding) + Insight die roll. Problem: clearing the clock isn't as noticeable as adding beads.

**NEW SYSTEM**: Insight die as turn token + time clock. When Insight die completes full circle back to GM = time passes, roll for random encounter.

**PROBLEM**: Insight normally changes once per Turn (when Turn Clock advances), but new system would change it once per player action. This creates a conflict with the core mechanic.

**SIMPLER ALTERNATIVE**: Roll every turn, random monster appears on "spicy" result.

**DEATH RULE SIMPLIFICATION**: Characters die when the encounter ends (not when turn clock runs out). Prevents players from being short-changed if they drop under 0 Hearts at the end of a turn. They still can't act when below 0 Hearts, just don't die until encounter resolution.

**FINAL SIMPLIFIED SYSTEM**:

- Ditch turn clock entirely
- Use metal bowl only for Insight die
- Roll for random encounter every turn using Oracle target
- **Simple 1-in-6 System**:
  - Roll 1d6 each turn
  - Wandering monster on 1
  - No bookkeeping, no complex tracking
  - Classic D&D rate, proven to work
  - Sometimes simple is best

**WANDERING MONSTER PROBLEM**: Oracle tables too random, producing deadly monsters (stone giants, ice golems) that don't fit the situation.

**SOLUTION: 2d6 Drop System**:

- Roll 2d6, drop higher die (1-6 range, weighted toward lower)
- **Doubles = trap** (pit, arrow, poison gas, etc.)
- **Noisy actions**: Roll twice (chance of multiple monsters)
- **Oracle with Advantage System**:
  - "Do they avoid a wandering monster?" with Advantage
  - **Target 8** with Advantage = 83.3% chance to avoid (16.7% encounter rate)
  - **All three dice** = individual monster HD (when encounter occurs)
- **Example**: Roll 2,3,4 = 7 (No, encounter occurs) → 3 monsters with 2HD, 3HD, 4HD
- **Spicy (any 1)**: Add all dice together = 1 powerful monster
- **Example**: Roll 1,3,4 = 8 (No, encounter occurs) → 1 monster with 8HD
  - **Advantage** reduces encounter odds while providing monster level info

**SOLUTION OPTIONS**:

1. **Separate Token**: Use a different object (coin, mini, etc.) for turn management, keep Insight die for its original purpose
2. **Fluid Insight**: Allow Insight to change more frequently (once per player action instead of once per Turn)
3. **Hybrid System**: Use Insight die for turns, but only roll for new Insight when the die completes a full circle
4. **Modified Turn Structure**: Redefine what constitutes a "Turn" in the new system

**RECOMMENDED SOLUTION: Insight Cards**

- Each player gets 6 Insight cards, one for each Insight facet:
  - 1 TRAIT card
  - 1 ITEM card  
  - 1 SKILL card
  - 1 NAME card
  - 1 ASSIST card
  - 1 EFFORT card
- Play one card to gain Advantage (must still qualify for that Insight type)
- Card is discarded after use
- Cards reset when camping
- Eliminates "what's current Insight?" confusion
- Gives players agency to save cards for important rolls
- Encourages table sharing since players want others to use their cards
- **Eliminates grumbling** about unwanted Insight facets (Name when they don't have one, Assist when they can't qualify, etc.)

## **PLAYER DOMINANCE ISSUE: Table Hogging**

Some players talk over quieter players and boss them around, even when using turns. Need to address this directly.

**SOLUTION: Turn Cards (Combined with Insight)**

- Each player gets a "turn card" each turn
- Player must play one card (either for Insight or discard unwanted card)
- More fluid than rigid token passing
- Players choose when to take their turn by playing a card
- Can use card for Insight OR discard a card they don't want
- Natural flow: "I play my TRAIT card for Insight" or "I discard my EFFORT card to take my turn"

**CONCERN**: Handing out turn cards each turn might slow play and create table clutter.

**REMOTE PLAY ISSUE**: Physical cards and turn clocks are harder to see on Zoom, creating accessibility problems for remote players.

## **SOLUTION: Camp vs Adventure Time Management**

### **Adventure Time (Active Play)**

- **Quick actions only** (under 2-3 minutes)
- Turn clock active - time pressure and danger
- Random encounters possible
- Focus on exploration, combat, immediate problem-solving
- Examples: "I cast this spell", "I check this door", "I drink this potion"
- **Defer complex experiments**: "I'm adding this experiment to my camp list" and move on

**CLEAR DEFINITIONS NEEDED**:

- **Short Turn**: Quick action (under 2-3 minutes), immediate resolution
- **Long Action**: Extended activity (over 2-3 minutes), requires camp time
- **GM Intervention**: "That's a long action, do you want to do that now or add it to your camp list?"
- **Simple Phrase**: "That sounds time-consuming, wait for camp?"

**EXAMPLES**:

- **Short**: Cast spell, drink potion, attack, climb wall, open door
- **Long**: Check for traps (10 min), study magic items, experiment with spells, research artifacts

### **Camp Time (Safe Experimentation)**

- **Extended activities** (anything longer than a few minutes)
- Turn clock paused - no time pressure
- No random encounters
- Safe space for detailed experimentation
- Examples: "I study this magic item for 30 minutes", "I experiment with spell combinations", "I research this artifact"

### **Session Structure**

- End sessions when returning to camp or entering new areas
- Reserve last 10-15 minutes for camp activities
- Start sessions with camp activities or immediate adventure hooks
- Use camp time for character development and item research

### **Benefits**

- Keeps adventure time fast-paced and engaging for all players
- Gives everyone equal opportunity for experimentation
- Prevents table hogging during active play
- Creates natural session breaks and cliffhangers
- Maintains danger and urgency during exploration

## **BLACK HACK INSPIRATION: Tension Clock Concept**

**Black Hack System Analysis**:

- **Secret rolls every 15 minutes** OR when characters linger/perform revealing actions
- **1d6 table with 6 results**: Creature, Nearby life, Faraway life, Light reduction, Stress, Fatigue
- **Creature tables** based on dungeon level + character level
- **More narrative** than mechanical - creates atmosphere and tension

**Potential Integration**:

- **Tension Clock** instead of Turn Clock
- **Builds tension** over time rather than strict turn tracking
- **Narrative triggers** (lingering, loud actions) increase tension
- **Variety of results** beyond just monsters (signs of life, environmental effects)
- **Secret rolls** create uncertainty and atmosphere
- **15-minute real-time intervals** - psychological pressure to keep moving, prevents endless discussion
