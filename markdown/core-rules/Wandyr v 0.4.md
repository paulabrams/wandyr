# Welcome to Wåndyr\! {#welcome-to-wåndyr!}

**Wåndyr** is an adventure game about a band of adventurers wandering through a pulp fantasy world. The focus is on exploration, following rumors, discovering new places, finding magic, treasure hunting, camping, singing, storytelling, cleverly overcoming impossible odds, and coming back alive with tales to tell. In the spirit of The Hobbit, Wåndyr celebrates the journey of going there and back again.

The stories and songs of your characters emerge during play, filled with wonder and surprises that even the Guide doesn't anticipate.

#### Sandbox

The Guide describes the world but doesn't create a story beforehand. The local area remains wide open and undefined, even if some aspects of the wider world are known. This "sandbox" style allows everyone at the table to discover the unexpected together.

The Guide uses dice rolls and random tables to determine what happens rather than telling a predetermined story. Each new discovery adds to the growing lore of your campaign world.

![][image3]

#### The Oracle

The **Oracle** answers any yes/no question instantly, often adding unexpected "sweet" or "spicy" flavor to keep adventures surprising. Both Guide and players consult the Oracle during play.

For deeper questions, the Oracle excels at "20 questions" to reveal mysteries piece by piece. The Guide should also use the random tables in the **Wåndyr World Oracle**.

# Starting a Game {#starting-a-game}

Wåndyr is a game played with friends. Select one person to be the Guide; the other people are players.

#### 1 \- Starting Location

The Guide describes the starting location for the adventure, such as a medieval tavern in a town on the borderlands. The Guide can place this into a larger fantasy world, or let that be a mystery for now.

#### 2 \- Create Characters

Each player needs three characters. A character has two Traits and two Skills, for example:  
Lindon: brave, strong | poleax, armor

![][image4]

#### 2 \- Select a Rumor

The Guide then lists a few rumors that hint about troublesome events in the area or interesting places to explore. The players choose a rumor to follow.

#### 3 \- Muster a Character

Each player rolls 2d6 Hearts for each of their characters and chooses one to “Muster” (play). From now on, each player becomes their mustered character. The Guide gives new characters 1-4 basic “hand me down” items for weapons, armor, tools, spellbooks, etc.

#### 4 \- Pack for the Journey

The Guide should provide a landmark or general directions so the path of the journey is clear, and provide or suggest basic travel supplies (e.g. food, water, traveling cloak). Players can swap items amongst themselves, but must leave unnecessary items behind with their other characters.

#### 5 \- Embark

Start play on the road. Each player plays their mustered character; the Guide handles everything else.

![][image5]  
Character Creation  
Characters start with two Traits and two Skills. Use your own words, but keep it clear and simple. Example:

Rook: agile, cunning | knife, (lockpicks)

“Rook” is “agile” and “cunning” (Traits). Rook knows how to use a knife and *secretly* *lockpicks* (Skills).

#### Traits

A Trait is a keyword that describes a personal quality, occupation, homeland, species, or magic school. Examples: agile, bold, strong, tough, intelligent, clever, sneaky. A Trait must be a single word, or a common compound word like *man-at-arms, horse-thief, second-story-man, or witch-hunter*.

#### Skills

A Skill is an item proficiency. Examples: dagger, longsword, bow, lockpicks, shield, armor,  type of animal, spellbook, wand, arcane staff, etc. Skills can be broad or narrow, but broad skills just cover the most basic items of that kind, so “sword” does not provide skill in a specialized sword type such as “greatsword”.

The Guide will provide 1-4 “hand me down” items to the character when they first Muster, usually selected by their Skills.

#### Human

Characters are human unless they take a Trait to indicate otherwise (elf, dwarf, halfling, elf-friend, etc.); this costs one Skill..

#### Magic

A magic-user *must* take a Trait for a magic school; this costs one Skill.

Vesper: scholar “illusione” | spellbook  
Hezerek: ambitious “Umbrakalla“ | orb  
Talin: elf-friend “Thaumaturgy” | \--

#### Religion

Characters can take Traits or Skills to reflect profoundly held views (pilgrim, pious, zealot, witch-burner, etc.) but this is not required.

#### Level Zero

Characters start at Level Zero as unproven, untested novices with no prior experience. Their Traits and Skills are Level 0 by default. However, the Guide may decide to grant Levels to Traits or Skills that appear to be limited in usefulness.

# The Muster {#the-muster}

*Ok boys, check your kit, check your weapons, it’s today. You get a pint and a chaser, no more. Let’s go.*

During the Muster, each player takes on the role of one character adventuring today. The Guide provides new characters with 1-4 basic “hand me down” items.

#### Hearts

Players roll 2d6 Hearts for each of their three characters, and choose one who answers the Muster and will be played today.

#### Item Slots

Each character has 10 Item Slots to hold gear they have “equipped” and can access and use easily.

Each slot can hold one small item (cloak, flask, tinderbox, flask, 1 day rations, 1 day water) or a bundle of tiny identical items (coin purse, 20 arrows, bundle of 3 torches, lockpicks, bowstrings, lute strings, or 3 days iron rations, etc.).

Armaments use 1-4 slots by size:

* **Weapons:** dagger 1, sword 2, halberd 3, pike 4  
* **Bows:** arrow quiver 1, bow 2, crossbow 3, arbalest 4  
* **Shields:** buckler 1, heater 2, round shield 3, tower shield 4  
* **Armor:** gauntlets 1, helm 2, gambeson 3, haubergeon 4

#### Baggage

Characters are strong and can carry things outside their 10 slots. This is called “Baggage”. Characters with Baggage suffer in dice rolls (q.v. Insight), and the Guide may worry about their safety when swimming, leaping, climbing, etc.

#### Move

Characters have a 12” Move; where 1” is 10 feet in tactical situations, or 1” is 1 mile on a road. Cut in half for each impediment, e.g. Baggage, off-road, bad terrain, darkness, etc.

#### Home Base

The characters are part of an “adventuring party” with a “home base”. Party Level adds to Hearts when Mustering from “home base”, but it starts at level zero. *They might be a mercenary company, adventurers guild, warband, family of rogues, etc, and their base could be a tavern, hideout, tower, shrine, ruined wizard tower, etc.*

## Camping {#camping}

When the adventuring party decides to stop and rest, they must choose whether to make a temporary camp or return home.  

Camping ends the game session for the characters; they exit play until the next Muster. With the game paused, players can ask questions about loot they found, take care of shopping, earn XP, and spend XP. Each “game night” always ends in camp so the next game can start cleanly with a Muster.

#### Comforts

The comforts of home make everything feel a little better. A wheel of cheese, a crusty bread, mead, wine, or a good smoke can make even the most dismal dungeon feel fine. A character who consumes a Comfort during Muster receives up to 2 extra Hearts. A 1-Heart comfort is 10 gp and uses 1 slot; a 2-Heart is 100 gp and 2 slots.

#### Music, Songs & Stories

Music, songs and stories are important because they make everything better. A character who knows the Name of a song or story (or poem, etc.) can perform that work well. A character with a Skill in a musical instrument can play well. A character who performs during Muster receives up to 2 Hearts, 1 for a Name, 1 for a Skill.

## Experience {#experience}

Characters can learn new Traits, Skills, and Names while in camp.

The Guide awards XP for *exploration* each time the characters make Camp. They earn 1-6 XP for following a rumor fully to its conclusion. They also earn 1 XP if they discover an important new location or magical feature, map a new region, or rescue captives. *XP is never awarded for fighting or looting treasure.*

#### Using Experience Points

Characters can gain a new Trait, Skill, or Name by paying XP equal to the new count of that kind of thing. So the 3rd Trait is 3 xp and the 5th Skill is 5 xp.

Traits, Skills, and Names start at Level 0\. Characters can level them up by paying XP equal to 1d6 plus the next Level. So gaining level 1 (from 0\) costs 1d6+1 xp. Gaining level 3 (from 2\) costs 1d6+3, etc.

*The group of players may vote to waive the d6 portion of the XP cost for Traits that feel underpowered or hard-won.*

#### Names

“Names” must be earned through deeds in play. They can be personal “bynames”, titles, epithets, or names of stories or songs that have a personal connection. A character can only spend XP on a Name that was earned during play.

#### Party Level

Party Level grants Hearts when mustering from “home base” and used for individual character Level when setting the Oracle target.

Characters can advance their Party Level to Level 1 by paying 2,000 gp; the cost doubles for each Level. The gold is spent on various improvements to their “home base”, furnishings, training materials, parties, etc, and exits play.

# Game Play {#game-play}

The game mostly consists of a conversation around the table, where people take turns to ask questions and attempt actions.

#### Facts

When a player asks a question about a *fact* yet unknown, the Guide asks the Oracle for truth:

* “Is this town on a waterway?”  
* "Is there a trail in the woods?"  
* "Is the sword magical?"  
* “Do they seem friendly?”

#### Actions

When a player takes an action, they ask the Oracle for the result:

* "I flee and climb a tree\!"  
* "I throw my spear"  
* "I parry with my shield\!"  
* “I cast my Illusione spell\!”

Conversations may resemble a game of “20 questions”, but the answer will be a surprise to everyone at the table.

## Ask the Oracle {#ask-the-oracle}

Speak a question such as “does my spear hit the foe?” and roll 2d6: if the total is 8 or higher the answer is “Yes”, otherwise "No".

#### Advantage/Disadvantage

With Advantage, roll 3d6 and keep the two best dice; on Disadvantage roll 3d6 and keep the two worst. Never remove a 1 or 6 from the roll.

A character has Advantage if they can claim Insight.

A character can have Disadvantages for poor positioning, being unarmed, in bad terrain, poor visibility, wounds, using a bow in close combat, etc.

Advantage cancels all Disadvantages. A character can also overcome one Disadvantage with a specific Trait to keep their Advantage. A Disadvantage "unarmed" could be overcome by a "wrestling" Trait.

#### Easy / Hard

The Guide sets the Oracle target number as easy (6), normal (8), hard (10), or extreme (12). Fooling a country bumpkin is easy (6), a guard average (8), a merchant hard (10+).

When possible, the Guide should compare Party Level to the opponent’s Level to objectively set the Oracle target number. Character Level is on the same general scale as levels or HD in the original roleplaying game.

#### Sweet and Spicy

If any die rolls 6 the answer is more “sweet”, and if any die rolls 1 the answer is more “spicy”. Never remove a 1 or 6 from the roll.

Example: A character is bow-sniping a sentry and takes time to set up the shot, making it easy (6). If it ends up “spicy” another guard might notice, or if “sweet” the guard uniform is spotless.

When rolling 3 dice, any die counts\! Thus, a roll can be sweet-and-spicy, double-spicy, or even triple-sweet\!

#### 20 Questions

The Oracle can be used to generate more nuanced answers by playing a game of “20 questions”. Often just a few questions will do.

## Turns {#turns}

The game uses Turns to make sure each player has a chance to speak. A Turn is the amount of time needed for a player to ask a question, move, or take an action. The amount of time in a Turn varies depending on context, ranging from days overland travel, to a few seconds in a violent encounter. Regardless, the game is always played in Turns.

#### Turn Clock

The Guide keeps a simple “Turn Clock” to mark the passage of time. A metal bowl and glass beads or other tokens is ideal. Advance the Clock by 1 at the start of each Turn or whenever the players do something time-consuming. Roll Insight at the same time. When the Clock reaches 6 the Guide clears the Clock and asks the Oracle for a random encounter or event.

# Magic {#magic}

Wåndyr is full of magic, and much of the game is about exploring and meddling with magical secrets.

## Magic-Users {#magic-users}

Magic is classified by “school”, such as Vitae (life), Elementalis, Thaumaturgy (control), Illusione (illusions & mental), Umbrakala (shadows & gates), or Necromantia.

A character with a Trait for a **Magic School** is a “magic-user” of that school, literate in its otherwise secret arcane magic language. All words are magical to a degree, and all true magic is made of words, runes, glyphs, or arcane script.

Magic is rated by “level”, and magic-users only understand magic up to their Level in the magic school. A 1st level magic-user understands 1st level magic from their school, and knows enough to dangerously meddle beyond that.

#### Spell Casting

Casting a spell requires at least one free hand and an item containing the spell such as a Spellbook, Spell Scroll, or Arcana. The magic-user must say the magic power words loudly and precisely. This may not be possible in every situation.

### ![][image8]

#### Magic Oracle

The player must always ask the Oracle if the spell casting outcome matches their *intention*. On “NO” *something* *unintended* happens (Sweet is close but flawed and Spicy is wildly different or dangerous) Ask the Oracle a question or two about what happened, and add to Lore.

#### Casting Cost

Casting a spell costs Hearts. This is equal to the Spell Level. Meddling beyond the character’s magic school level may involve additional risks (or d6 Hearts).

Consuming a *Magic Component* related to a spell offsets casting cost by 1 Heart per item slot. Search for magic components in strange magical places.

#### Duration

Spell duration is Instant (zero time) or Active (multiple Turns). Active spells persist until the Turn Clock runs out. The caster may use an Action and pay 1 Heart to extend it for another Turn Clock.

#### Spellbooks

Common spellbooks are “1st level”. They use 1 item slot and have one random 1st level spell.

Rare spellbooks are larger: 2nd level spellbooks use 2 slots with one 2nd level spell and 2 1st level spells. 3rd level spellbooks use 3 item slots with one 3rd level spell and 2 2nd level spells.

#### Spell Scrolls

Spell Scrolls are short spells that fit 5 per item slot. Scrolls crumble to dust when used to cast the spell.

#### Arcana

Arcana are pieces of magic-user flair. An Arcana is an item using 1-4 slots by size (drippy ring 1, magic wand 2, wizard staff 3, big hat and robe 4). Arcana are helpful items for casting spells and can be any kind of item, even musical instruments, armaments, or magic tattoos, but only function when used *visibly*.

#### Enchanting

A magic-user can *enchant* an Arcana with a spell from a spellbook, enabling them to use the Arcana to cast the spell. The magic-user must pay the spell level in XP (increases if total spell levels exceed item slots). The words of power move to the Arcana, leaving behind a spell fragment. The spell also affects the “drip” of the Arcana.

#### Weapon Arcana

Weapon Arcana (*e.g. rod, staff, wand, rune sword, magic bow, etc*.) are usable as weapons. The magic-user can also cast a spell on a “hit” instead of dealing damage.

## Magic Items {#magic-items}

All magic items bear mystical inscriptions—sharp dwarven runes, flowing elven script, or shifting arcane glyphs. Only those versed in magic can decipher these writings.

Most enchanted items function as their mundane counterparts but with supernatural enhancements. Elven boots silence footsteps, while a hero's belt bestows strength—typically manifesting as Traits or Skills. Anyone wielding such items may discover these benefits through use.

#### Magic “Plus”

Some items have a magic “plus”. Defensive items such as armor and shields provide the “plus” as Hearts. Magic weapons increase Hearts dealt as damage. For example, *full mail \+1 (4 slots)* gives 5 Hearts and a *sword \+2* deals d6+2 Hearts.

#### Power Words (Arcana)

Items which bear magic power words to cast spells are *Arcana*.

#### Artifacts

The most potent magical artifacts bear a True Name, binding the soul of a magic-user, elemental, *d'jinn, ifr'it, kha'din*, dragon, eldar, deep one, or other entity. These bound spirits are notoriously capricious, jealous, and unhinged from their confinement. Their original dreams and ambitions may persist or have been twisted by madness. Artifacts are temperamental; when a character first attempts to use one, ask the Oracle if the item \*accepts\* them. On Sweet or Spicy, the item also talks to them\!

A character who learns a True Name grows in *alignment* and may discover deeper mysteries. It’s petty, but they *hate* anyone who offends their fragile egos, such as carrying them after being rejected, refusing to follow their agenda, abandoning, or "cheating" on them with another item.

## Invocations {#invocations}

A character who knows the *True* *Name* of an otherworldly being can *call* them. *The character does not need to take this as one of their Names.*

#### Lore

When a Name is first found, randomly generate it. The Guide rolls the Oracle to answer any questions such as “is this Name known in this age of the world?” or “is this Name aligned with Law?”. Anything learned becomes part of the known Lore of the Name, which can help shape future interactions.

#### Invocations

A character makes an Invocation by loudly and formally calling the Name *thrice* and making a request.

The player rolls the Oracle to ask if the request is fulfilled. On Sweet the being *likes* the request, and on Spicy they *dislike* it. The character loses d6 Hearts (half on Sweet, double on Spicy). If they run out of Hearts something weird happens.

If the Oracle says the request is fulfilled, the Guide follows existing Lore and asks the Oracle how that plays out. Character alignment to the Name matters.

#### Alignment

After an Invocation, the character must offer 1-6 XP to the Name and ask the Oracle how they are spent.

#### Relics

Relics are objects with connections to otherworldly beings. They are conduits for alignment, and have even been known to make Invocations on behalf of their possessors. Relics are often mistaken for magic items or as “cursed” magic items.

# Monsters {#monsters}

The Guide can use monsters from the original roleplaying game, or “wing it” with this simple method. Each monster has a **Level** and the Oracle is “hard” if this is higher than Party Level; or “easy” if lower.

**Level 0:** giant rat, vermin, goblin  
**Level 1:** orc, skeleton, deathsquito  
**Level 2:** zombie, ghoul, giant ant, slime  
**Level 3:** wight, harpy, giant spider  
**Level 4:** werewolf, ogre, huge slime  
**Level 5:** mummy, minotaur, owlbeast  
**Level 6:** troll, wyvern, spectre, manticore  
**Level 7:** griffon, djinni, vampire  
**Level 9:** chimera, giant  
**Level 10:** golem, dragon

Weak monsters have d6 Hearts; leaders 2d6. A solitary strong monster has 3d6 Hearts per HD, multiple attacks, plus a deadly attack (3d6, d6 poison for multiple Turns, petrify, paralysis, etc).

# Fighting {#fighting}

A character can take one action while moving each Turn.

#### Attack

When a character attacks, the player asks the Oracle if they “hit” and deal d6 Hearts.

#### Defend

When a character is attacked, the player asks the Oracle if they defend. If they are “hit” the Guide rolls Hearts lost (usually d6). Characters can use “iron” Hearts from armor and shields against weapons; they reset in Camp.

#### Hearts

*A character is not considered injured until they run out of Hearts.*

A character at 0 Hearts is injured but can fully recover in Camp. A character below 0 Hearts is in *critical* condition and needs magic healing. When the Turn Clock clears, anyone below 0 Hearts immediately dies.

![][image11]

#### Initiative

The Guide determines the “initiative order” in which characters and monsters take their turns.

Attacks and counter attacks are often simultaneous. However, when enemies approach from a distance to fight, the weapon with the better reach has the “right of way” to attack first (when in doubt use item slots). Smaller melee weapons are better in close quarters combat.

#### Melee

A character within 1” (10 feet) of an enemy at any point of time in the current Turn is “in Melee”. If they do not attack with a close combat weapon (e.g. dagger, sword, spear, pistol) they have Disadvantage on *ALL* rolls this turn, and enemies may even interrupt their action. For example, a bow or spellbook are simple to interrupt.

#### Saving Throws

When someone is exposed to a harmful effect (magic, poison, paralysis, petrification, breath, falling, etc.), ask the Oracle if they somehow avoid the “worst half” of the effect. On YES they take half the effect, such as half damage, half duration, half petrification, etc. The Guide sets the Oracle target (by comparing character level with spell level or monster level).

# The Oracle {#the-oracle}

Ask a question and roll 2d6: if the total is 8 or higher the answer is “Yes”, otherwise "No".

Phrase the question for yes/no where the character wants “Yes”:

* "I wonder if I can determine if the sword is magical?"  
* "Does my spear hit the monster?"  
* "Do I dodge the boulder?

#### Sweet & Spicy

If any die rolls 6 the answer is “Sweet”, and if any die rolls a 1 the answer is “Spicy”. The player asks a question on Sweet; the Guide asks a question on Spicy.

The Oracle also knows how to play 20 questions.

# Insight {#insight}

Insight is an opportunity to claim advantage. Players who follow Insight on a roll have Advantage. Roll d6 for Insight each Turn.

#### 1 \- TRAIT

Use a helpful Trait. Strong helps open stuck doors or crack heads.

#### 2 \- ITEM

Use a helpful item. (Special items require a specialized item Skill, e.g. longbow, lockpicks, greatsword, spellbooks, kinds of arcana, etc.)

#### 3 \- SKILL

Use a helpful item Skill.

#### 4 \- NAME

Use a relevant Name. (A Name can be a song, story, named item, character title, etc.). “Dragon Slayer” helps with dragon stuff.

#### 5 \- ASSIST

Use an Action previously taken that “sets up” or “assists” the current roll. A “parry” helps defend. Characters can assist each other if they sequence their actions.

#### 6 \- EFFORT

Apply more effort. Automatic unless limiting effort or already at max effort. Examples: making something look easy, being quiet or stealthy, climbing, swimming, wrestling a bear, or carrying **Baggage**.
