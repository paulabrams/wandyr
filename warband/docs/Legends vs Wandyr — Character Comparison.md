# Legends vs Wandyr — Character Comparison

*Research behind the Warband character-sheet decision — how characters work in Legends of the Dragon Isles and Wåndyr. Split out of [[Warband]].*

Both games share the same core premise: **a character sheet with no numbers — you describe the character in words.** Both treat the character as a *novice* whose claims are aspirational. They diverge in how much structure the sheet carries and what dice drive play.

**Legends of the Dragon Isles** — a 7-part sheet, more narrative slots:

1. **Name** + epithet/alias ("Drayondir the Swift")
2. **Theme** — a single role/class *or* ancestry, with a tagline (Fighter, Magic-User, Cleric, Thief, Eldar; plus Homeland & Look). This is a dedicated identity slot.
3. **Traits** — two qualities/common skills (strong, patient, climbing…)
4. **Equipment** — five common items
5. **Asset** — one prized possession with an *Asset Signature* that grants a [+] upgrade on one kind of roll (e.g. "silver mace, burns undead")
6. **Secrets** — two special skills/powers known only to you (magic ones cost 1 Vim)
7. **Dark Secret** — one hidden aspect of your past

*Dice:* d20, succeed on 14+, crit-fail on 3–. A **Legend Die** (d6 = "TEACUP": Theme, Effort, Asset, Combo, Ult, Prep) upgrades a result one step when you meet the criteria. Combat uses Doom/damage (3 strikes → Down) and a Weapon-vs-Armor class comparison. Hazard & Stress numbers shift with dramatic tension.

**Wåndyr** — a minimal one-line sheet:

- Format: **`Alias: traits | skills`** (e.g. "Rook: agile, cunning | knife, lockpicks")
- **Traits** = *how* you play (behavior/soft skills; can fold in ancestry like Dwarf/Elf)
- **Skills** = *what* you know (hard skills — item proficiency or knowledge, including magic schools)
- **Hearts:** roll 2d6 per character; you create **three** characters and "Muster" (play) one each session
- **10 Item Slots** + optional **Baggage** for overflow

*Dice:* 2d6 **Oracle**, success on 8+ (range 6–12 easy→extreme). **Sweet & Spicy** twists on any 6 or 1; an **Insight** die each round/turn.

**Both have rich advancement — this is the key thing they share, not where they differ.**

- **Legends** grows *every slot on the sheet.* A d6 advancement roll (the TEACUP categories) lets you add/refine a Theme, Trait, Asset Signature, Secret, or Dark Secret. Secrets chain into stronger Secrets and then into five **Ritual** tiers (Field → Circle → Node → High → Aeonic). You can gain **Minions** (Retainers, Familiars), and **Transform** into Eldar ancestral forms that carry Innate Assets. Some ancestries have their own tracks (Karrock consume gems on EFFORT, Vôzen quaff potions). "Homework" lets a player *choose* an advance instead of rolling.
- **Wåndyr** grows the *traits/skills line and its attachments.* You earn XP from Points of Interest (a 6-factor difficulty rating) and spend **3 XP** per pick on Traits, Skills, Bynames, Songs, Recipes, Known Spells, Arcana, Relics, or Knacks. **Alignment** rises as you accumulate themed advances (count ÷ 3), unlocking Party-Leader/Hero perks.

The difference is *shape*, not *depth*: Legends advancement fans out across many sheet dimensions and into transformation/minions/rituals; Wandyr advancement is a single XP economy feeding a long menu of pick-ups.

**Key differences**

| Aspect | Legends | Wandyr |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Role/class | Dedicated **Theme** slot | Folded into **Traits** |
| Sheet depth | 7 parts (Asset, Secrets, Dark Secret add hooks) | 1 line (traits + skills) |
| Core die | d20, 14+ | 2d6 Oracle, 8+ |
| "Luck" mechanic | Legend Die upgrade (TEACUP) | Sweet/Spicy + Insight |
| Inventory | 5 items | 10 slots + Baggage |
| Character count | One | Make 3, Muster 1 (Hearts) |
| Advancement | Rich (every sheet slot grows; Secrets→Rituals, Transformation, Minions) | Rich (XP→Traits/Skills/Bynames/Songs/Recipes/Spells/Arcana/Relics/Knacks; Alignment) |
| Advancement driver | d6 advancement roll (TEACUP) refines a slot; "Homework" lets you pick | Spend 3 XP per pick; XP from Points of Interest |

**Implication for Warband:** Wandyr's traits/skills line and item slots are the lean base (already on the want-list), while Legends contributes the **Asset** and **Secrets/Dark Secret** depth and the Theme→Approach swap. Because *both* games already advance richly, the want-list item "rich character development and advancement" is less about inventing a new system than about **choosing which advancement shape to keep**: Legends' fan-out across many sheet slots (with chained Secrets/Rituals/Transformation) or Wandyr's single XP economy spent against a long menu — or a hybrid where a Wandyr-style XP spend buys Legends-style Secrets/Assets. The open question for the Priority Queue is which slots, and which advancement engine, survive into a single Warband sheet.
