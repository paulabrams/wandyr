# The Tension Pool

The Tension Pool adds a sense of rising tension to your game. The Tension Pool also increases the players' awareness of the passage of in-game time. And it adds a sense of weight to time-wasting and reckless actions.

## Setting Up the Tension Pool

You will need a bowl (ideally glass or metal) and six dice.

1. **Tension Pool** - Put the bowl middle of the table where everyone can see it.
2. **Tension Dice** - Put the six dice where you can reach them and easily and loudly toss them into the Tension Pool.

## Using the Tension Pool

Whenver the players take an action, you'll need to decide whether to add a die to the Tension Pool, roll the Tension Pool, or do both. You'll also periodically clear the Tension Pool. After rolling the Tension Pool, you'll often introduce a Complication in the adventure.

- **Each 15 minutes of real time**: Add a Tension Die
- **Whenever the players are loitering**: Add a Tension Die
- **Whenever a player commits to a Time-Consuming action**: Add a Tension Die
- **Whenever a player commits to a Reckless action**: Roll the Tension Pool
- **Whenever a player commits to an action that's both Time-Consuming and Reckless**: First Add a Tension Die and then Roll the Tension Pool
- **Whenever you add the sixth Tension Die to the Tension Pool**: Clear the Tension Pool
- **Whenever a player describes a Side Action**: Ask them if they want to save it for later, otherwise add a Tension Die

Most actions are neither Time-Consuming nor Reckless. Also ignore the Tension Pool during battles, chases, negotiations, and other action-packed encounters. The Tension Pool is only for managing the action during investigation, exploration, and other slow parts of the game where the players tend to sink time. You may also want to suggest players save Side Actions for later to keep the game moving.

### Adding Tension Dice

During exploration, investigation, and other slow-paced parts of the game, whenever a player commits their character to a Time-Consuming action, add one Tension Die to the Tension Pool. It's as simple as picking up one of the Tension Dice and dropping it loudly into the container so everyone knows what happened.

#### Time-Consuming Actions

Use your best judgement about what constitutes Time-Consuming. As a rule, any action that takes a long amount of time given the current game action is Time Consuming.

While the players are delving a dungeon or infiltrating an enemy base, most actions play out in seconds. A minute at the most. Any action that takes a few minutes to play out is Time-Consuming.

While the players are playing detective in town, most actions play out in an hour or some fraction thereof. Any action that takes a few hours to resolve is Time Consuming.

Don't add multiple Tension Dice to the Tension Pool if several players commit to Time-Consuming actions at the same time. However, if a player takes an especially long, complex Time-Consuming action, you can add two, three, or more Tension Dice to the Tension Pool.

#### Saving Side Actions

A Side Action is a tangental action that is interesting to a single player, but not the entire group. Too many Side Actions can cause player to become bored or lose focus and turn to their screens. For example, one player might want to experiment with a magic item they found, but that takes time and might be boring for some players.

When a player mentions a Side Action during the game, any player can suggest they "save it" for later. If the player agrees, don't add a Tension Die to the Tension Pool. The player is responsible for bringing up the saved Side action later, such as in-between game sessions. Until they do so, the action hasn't yet occurred.

If the group discovers a saved Side Action is actually immediately relevant to the current game play, then resume the action and add a Tension Die to the Tension Pool.

### Rolling the Tension Pool

During exploration, investigation, and other slow-paced parts of the game, whenever a player commits to a Reckless action, roll the Tension Pool and check for a Complication.

1. Pick up all the Tension Dice currently in the Tension Pool and roll them
2. If there are no Tension Dice in the Tension Pool, pick up one Tension Die and roll it
3. If any of the dice shows a 1, a Complication arises
4. Put the Tension Dice back in the Tension Pool

#### Reckless Actions

Use your best judgement about what constitutes a Reckless action. Generally, Reckless actions are those that ignore danger, risk serious immediate consequences, attract unwanted attention, or involve a high risk for a fast and easy result.

While the players are delving or infiltrating, Reckless actions might include having noisy arguments, breaking doors, fighting, being careless about light, or spreading odors of food or blood.

While the players are playing detective, Reckless actions might include threatening informants, carelessly sharing information about the investigation, or breaking into crime scenes on public streets.

### Clearing the Tension Pool

Whenever you add the sixth Tension Die to the Tension Pool, immediately roll the Tension Pool. After determining whether a Complication arises, put the Tension Dice aside and start all over with an empty Tension Pool.

Always roll the Tension Pool to check for a Complication when you clear it. Never take the Tension Dice out of the Pool without rolling them.
