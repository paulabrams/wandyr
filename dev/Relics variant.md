## The Idea

Every relic contains a being â€” or a piece of one. A
saint's finger holds a fragment of Athyr-Vok. A black
sword holds the spirit of Morthul. A jade scarab holds
something older than either. The difference is not what
the object is, but whether the being inside is a
prisoner, a guest, or a piece of something larger.

Relics do not grant wishes. They make **offers**.

## What Is a Relic?

A Relic is any object that contains a will â€” a bound
spirit, a fragment of an otherworldly being, a trapped
elemental, a sliver of divine attention, or something
stranger. To the uninitiated, they may appear cursed,
haunted, or merely old.

All Relics share these properties:

- They have (or are part of) a **being** with a nature
  and agenda
- They make **offers** â€” revealing powers and demanding
  costs
- They **shape** those who carry them â€” nudging the
  bearer toward Traits that reflect the being's nature
- They deepen through **Approach** â€” the more aligned
  you become, the more the Relic reveals

### Kinds of Relics

**Sacred Relics** contain a piece or conduit of an
otherworldly being â€” a bone, a staff, an inkpot. The
being speaks through the object but exists beyond it.
The Relic is a phone line. Destroying it wounds the
connection but not the being.

**Sentient Relics** contain the being itself â€” trapped,
bound, or dwelling within by choice. The object *is*
the will. Destroying it may kill or free whatever lives
inside.

**Sleeping Relics** contain latent magic with no
apparent will â€” yet. Some may awaken through use. Some
may never speak. These are ordinary magic items until
they aren't.

The distinction matters for the fiction but not the
mechanics. All Relics use the same framework.

## Offers

Relics do not respond to requests. They **offer** â€”
revealing what they are willing to give and what they
want in return.

### How Offers Work

When you carry a Relic and the moment is right â€” a
crisis, a quiet night by the fire, a surge of emotion,
a moment of need â€” the Relic may make an offer.

The Guide determines when an offer occurs, using any
of these triggers:

- You use the Relic in a new or significant way
- Your Approach with the being increases
- You roll Sweet or Double Sweet while carrying it
- The story demands it
- You call the being's True Name (see Invocations)

When an offer triggers, the Guide rolls on the Relic's
**Discovery Table** to determine what is revealed, and
on the **Shaping Table** to determine the cost.

### Accepting or Refusing

**Accept**: You gain the offered power. You also accept
the cost â€” a Trait, a restriction, a hunger, a change.
Your Approach with the being deepens.

**Refuse**: You keep what you have. The Relic may
remember. Some offers do not come twice. The being's
disposition toward you may shift.

## Invocations

If you know a being's True Name â€" whether it lives in
a Relic or beyond one â€" you can invoke it. Call the
True Name thrice. Lose d6 Hearts.

You may leave it at that â€" a bare summons, a knock on
the door. Or you may say more: a request, an entreaty,
a supplication, a devotion, a confession, a threat, a
question, a song. You can say anything. The being hears
all of it.

Ask the Oracle if the being responds.

- **Yes**: The being makes an offer. If your words match
  something specific on the Discovery Table, the Guide
  may simply choose it. Otherwise, the Guide rolls on
  the Discovery Table and Shaping Table.
- **No**: Silence. The Hearts are still spent.
- **Sweet**: The offer is generous â€" lower cost, greater
  power, or an unexpected gift.
- **Spicy**: The offer has a hook â€" a steep cost, an
  unwanted Trait, or a demand.

The player then accepts or refuses as normal.

### Invocations Without a Relic

You can invoke a being without carrying their Relic.
The connection is weaker â€” the Guide may set the Oracle
harder â€” but the framework is the same. The being
offers; you accept or refuse.

### Invocations With a Sentient Relic

Sentient Relics may respond to their True Name without
the full invocation ritual. They are right there in
your hand. A whispered name, a surge of will, a
moment of need â€” the Guide decides when this counts
as an invocation and when it's just the item talking.

## Omens

Relics whisper. Before they offer power, before they
reveal their nature, they give **omens** â€” small signs,
nudges, warnings. A chill up the spine. Ink that moves
on its own. A blade that hums near danger. A ring that
tightens on your finger when you're being lied to.

Omens are the Relic's way of communicating without
words. They cost nothing. They demand nothing. They
are the being saying: *pay attention*.

Omens foretell both **Offers** and **Compulsions**.
A sword that hums near undead is hinting at a power
it might offer. A chalice that makes your skin crawl
with dread is warning you about what it will demand.
Both are information for the player to decide what
to do â€” pick it up, put it down, carry it further,
or leave it behind.

**Omens are player-facing.** They exist so the player
can make informed choices. A character who ignores an
omen and picks up the chaos chalice anyway has chosen
their fate. A character who heeds the warning and
walks away has dodged it. Either is a good story.

### Hidden Tables

When a Relic first becomes significant in play, the
Guide generates four hidden d6 tables specific to
that Relic. The player never sees the tables â€” only
the results of their rolls.

The four tables are:

- **Omens (d6)** — Six specific signs the Relic gives
- **Discoveries (d6)** — Six specific powers, gated by Approach
- **Traits (d6)** — Six Traits the being wants to shape in you
- **Cost (d6)** — Six demands, restrictions, or compulsions

The tables serve double duty â€” they tell the Guide
what the Relic *is*. If the Traits table reads
"hungry, wrathful, possessive," the Guide knows how
to roleplay the Relic's omens and offers consistently.
The hidden tables are the Relic's character sheet.

### Omen Table (d6)

Six specific ways this Relic communicates. Each entry
describes a concrete, sensory sign â€” not a category.

Good entries name what the player *perceives*:

- "The runes flare white when undead are near"
- "The ink writes a single word you didn't choose"
- "The blade grows heavier the closer you get"
- "The scarab vibrates against your chest, then stops"
- "The ring turns ice-cold on your finger"
- "Complete silence â€” the ambient sound drops away"

The player rolls when the Guide determines the Relic
is reacting to something. The same entry may trigger
multiple times in different contexts â€” the sign is
the same, but the meaning shifts with the situation.

### When to Give Omens

Omens are not gated by Approach level. A Level 0
bearer gets omens. This is how the relationship
begins â€” the Relic reaches out before you understand
what it is.

The Guide gives omens:

- When the bearer enters a significant location
- When danger is near but undetected
- When something relevant to the being is present
- When the player is about to make a choice the being
  cares about
- When the story needs a nudge

Omens are free. They are the hook that makes the player
curious enough to deepen the Approach and earn the
real offers.

## Discovery Table (d6)

Six specific powers this Relic can reveal. Each entry
names a concrete ability with a trigger condition.

Good entries are specific and playable:

- "Cuts through magical bindings as if they were rope
  (always, while drawn)"
- "Cast *Death Ward* as Arcana (1/Muster)"
- "On a killing blow, the blade drinks â€” restore 1
  Heart (on kill)"
- "Shows you the last memory of anyone whose blood
  touches it (use)"
- "Weighs nothing when worn; no swim or climb penalty
  (always, while worn)"
- "Whispers the true name of anyone you touch with the
  blade (1/Muster, on touch)"

Discoveries are gated by **Approach level**:

- **Approach 0** — 1 discovery (the first sign)
- **Approach 1** — 2–3 discoveries
- **Approach 2** — 4–5 discoveries
- **Approach 3+** — All 6

The player rolls when a discovery triggers. If they
roll an entry above their current gate, nothing
happens â€” the Relic isn't ready to show them yet.
They may reroll at the next trigger.

The Guide decides which entries correspond to which
tiers when generating the table. Early discoveries are
subtle; later ones are dramatic.

## Traits Table (d6)

Six specific Traits the being wants to shape in you.
Each reflects the being's personality and values.

Good entries form a coherent portrait:

- Athyr-Vok: patient, perceptive, curious, truthful,
  humble, secretive
- Bonereaver: wrathful, hungry, proud, merciless,
  restless, commanding
- Jade Cicada: silent, watchful, cold, precise,
  solitary, deathward

The Guide rolls on the Traits table when an offer is
made:

- If you already have the Trait: harmony. No cost.
  The being is pleased.
- If you don't: the being offers to shape you. Accept
  the Trait (costs 3 XP) or refuse the offer entirely.

**Sweet offers**: the Trait feels like a gift. "Patient"
when you needed patience. A natural fit.

**Spicy offers**: the Trait feels like a compulsion.
"Wrathful" when you wanted to be calm. The being is
pushing you somewhere you didn't choose.

---

## Cost Table (d6)

Six specific demands, restrictions, or behavioral
compulsions the being imposes. These are the price
of the relationship beyond Traits.

Good entries are concrete and create play:

- "You must carry the Relic in an Item Slot â€” always"
- "You cannot benefit from another being's Relics"
- "You must speak the truth when asked a direct
  question"
- "You hunger â€” provisions restore nothing unless you
  feed the blade first"
- "You must enter the next door you find, even if you
  shouldn't"
- "Companions grow uneasy in your presence; hospitality
  restores 1 fewer Heart"

The Guide rolls on the Cost table alongside the Traits
table. The combination defines the full offer:

- **Traits** = what you become
- **Cost** = what you must do

Sweet offers have lighter costs. Spicy offers have
heavier ones. Double spicy â€” the cost is steep and
non-negotiable. Refuse and the Relic may withdraw
entirely.

Costs persist as long as the relationship deepens.
Some may be lifted if you refuse an offer and accept
the consequences. The Guide tracks active costs.

## Compulsions

Some Relics do not ask. They **compel**.

A Compulsion is shaping that happens whether you accept
it or not â€” the cost of carrying something with a will
stronger than yours. Where an Offer says "I will give
you this if you become that," a Compulsion says "you
will become this because you carry me."

### How Compulsions Work

When you carry a Relic with a Compulsion, the Guide
rolls on the Relic's **Compulsion Table** at triggers
such as:

- Each Muster while carrying the Relic
- When you defy the being's nature or agenda
- When you use the Relic's power at high cost
- On Spicy or Double Spicy while carrying it

The Compulsion manifests as a behavioral demand â€” a
hunger, an obsession, a prohibition, a change in
temperament. It is not a Trait you choose. It is a
Trait that chooses you.

### Resisting Compulsions

You can resist a Compulsion by asking the Oracle. The
Guide sets the target based on the being's strength,
how long you've carried the Relic, and your Approach
level (higher Approach may make resistance easier *or*
harder, depending on the being).

**Success**: You hold yourself together. For now.

**Failure**: The Compulsion takes hold. You gain the
compelled Trait â€” or act on the compelled behavior â€”
this Muster.

**Putting it down**: You can always choose to stop
carrying the Relic. Some Compulsions fade when you do.
Some don't. The Guide decides based on how deep the
relationship has gone.

### Compulsion Tables (d6)

Each dangerous Relic has its own table. Examples:

**Bonereaver (Black Rune Blade)**

1. **Hunger** — you crave violence; peace feels wrong
2. **Whispers** — the blade speaks and you listen
3. **Jealousy** — you resent anyone who touches it
4. **Cruelty** — mercy feels like weakness
5. **Isolation** — companions annoy you; solitude calls
6. **Thirst** — the blade wants blood and you feel dry

**Chaos Chalice**

1. **Servitude** — you feel an urge to serve the temple
2. **Paranoia** — allies seem untrustworthy
3. **Greed** — you covet what others have
4. **Blasphemy** — sacred things disgust you
5. **Recklessness** — caution feels cowardly
6. **Visions** — the temple's god shows you its glory

### Omens Foretell Compulsions

This is why Omens matter. Before you pick up the chaos
chalice, it radiates dread. Before Bonereaver's hunger
takes hold, the runes brighten at the smell of blood.
The Relic warns you. The question is whether you
listen.

A well-played Omen gives the player everything they
need to make an informed choice:

- *"The chalice is warm to the touch. Your hand
  trembles. Something vast and hungry is aware of
  you."* â€” The player knows this is dangerous.
- *"The blade hums when the prisoner begs for mercy.
  It sounds pleased."* â€” The player knows what kind
  of thing they carry.

The player who picks up the chalice anyway has made a
**choice**, not suffered a gotcha. That's the
difference between a curse and a story.

## Approach and Relics

Your Approach with a being determines:

- **What discoveries are available** (gated by level)
- **How generous the offers are** (Guide uses Approach
  to calibrate)
- **Maximum effect level** for powers that cost Hearts
- **Saint bonus** if you qualify (see Saints below)

Approach level is calculated normally â€” count all
relevant advancements, divide by 3, round down.
Advancements that count toward a being's Approach
include:

- Traits shaped by the being
- Skills used in service of the being's nature
- Bynames earned while carrying the Relic
- Spells learned through the Relic
- The Relic itself (each Relic counts as 1 advancement)
- Other Relics of the same being

---

## Saints

If you Muster carrying only Relics of a single being
(no other Relics or Sentient items), you are that
being's **Saint**. You gain extra Hearts at Muster
equal to the lower of your Approach level and the
number of Relics in Item Slots.

Saints may also bless companions to pursue a rumor the
being favors. Each companion who accepts gains extra
Hearts (lower of Approach level and Relics in Slots).
On failure to complete the rumor, the being may claim
and shape their spirits.

## Sleeping Relics (Magic Items)

Not every magic item talks. Most are Sleeping Relics â€”
objects with enchantments but no apparent will.

Sleeping Relics still use the Discovery framework. You
learn what they do through use, experiment, and Skill.
Your magic school Approach gates what you can discover.
But there is no Shaping, no offers, no costs beyond
what the magic itself demands.

Some Sleeping Relics awaken. A sword carried through
enough battles might develop opinions. A ring worn for
years might start to whisper. When this happens, the
Guide creates a Shaping Table and the item becomes a
Sentient Relic.

---

## Wondrous Places

Places touched by magic â€” sacred groves, cursed
barrows, standing stone circles â€” are **Wonders**.
They work like Relics you cannot carry.

A Wonder may:

- Grant bonuses to certain schools within its bounds
- Make offers to those who linger or perform rituals
- Have a Discovery Table revealed through exploration
- Shape those who dwell there too long

A Wonder with a will and a True Name is effectively a
being that cannot move. Invoke it as you would any
other.

## Summary

**Sacred Relic** — Being speaks through it. Omens: yes. Makes offers: yes (being's agenda). Discovery table: yes. Traits table: yes. Cost table: yes. Invocation: yes (being's name). Approach: being's Approach.

**Sentient Relic** — Being lives inside. Omens: yes. Makes offers: yes (its own agenda). Discovery table: yes. Traits table: yes. Cost table: yes. Invocation: yes (item's name). Approach: dedicated Approach.

**Sleeping Relic** — Not yet (maybe). Omens: sometimes. Makes offers: no. Discovery table: yes. Traits table: no. Cost table: no. Invocation: no. Approach: school Approach. Can awaken: yes.

**Wonder** — Sometimes has a will. Omens: sometimes. Makes offers: sometimes. Discovery table: yes. Traits table: sometimes. Cost table: sometimes. Invocation: if it has a name. Approach: varies.

## Design Notes

This variant merges the prior Sentient Items and Relics
rules into a single framework. The key ideas:

1. **Offers, not wishes.** Beings reveal what they're
   willing to give. The player accepts or refuses.
   This replaces the open-ended "make a request" model.

2. **Omens before offers.** Relics communicate through
   small signs first â€” warnings, pulls, moods, silence.
   This is how the relationship begins, before the
   bearer understands what they carry. Omens are free,
   ungated, and the hook that draws players deeper.
   Critically, omens foretell both offers *and*
   compulsions â€” giving the player informed agency
   before they commit.

3. **Compulsions, not gotchas.** Dangerous relics compel
   behavior â€” but only after omens have warned the
   player. A curse you chose is a story. A curse that
   blindsides you is bad game design.

4. **Discovery, not negotiation.** Powers emerge through
   play and Approach level, not through player requests.
   The truth of the Relic reveals itself over time.

5. **All relics are relationships.** Whether the being
   is a god, a ghost, or a sword â€” the mechanic is the
   same. Carry it, use it, learn what it offers, decide
   what you're willing to become.

6. **Sleeping relics can awaken.** This preserves the
   classic "magic item" feel while leaving the door
   open for deeper stories.

7. **Wonders extend the framework to places.** A sacred
   grove and a sacred sword work the same way.
