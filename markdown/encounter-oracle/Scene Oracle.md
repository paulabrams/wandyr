# Scene Oracle

Generate 6 theater-of-the-mind scene variations from a location/encounter summary.

## Scene Structure

Each variation includes 6 elements: Summary, Shadow, Light, Loot, Aside, and Stats. The ranges are based on visibility and narrative flow rather than specific distances, adapting to light sources and environmental conditions.

**1. Summary:** One-sentence description of the scene, followed by the scene difficutly level (inferred from OSR hit dice 0-20, but focusing on levels 0-5 except on request).

**2. Shadow:** Mystery and anticipation

- **Establishing Shot**: Wide view of the location's character and atmosphere
- **Foreshadowing**: Plant visual/audio clues that hint at what's coming
- **Mood Lighting**: Use light/dark, color temperature, and shadows to set tone
- **Sound Design**: Ambient audio that tells a story (distant sounds, echoes, silence)
- **Visual Metaphors**: Use shapes and forms that suggest themes (spirals for mystery, sharp angles for danger)
- Examples: "distant archway silhouetted against moonlight," "rhythmic dripping echoing from the depths"

**3. Light:** Revelation and clarity

- **Medium Shot**: Focus on specific elements that advance the story
- **Character Reveals**: Introduce threats, NPCs, or important objects
- **Pacing Control**: Use rhythm and timing to build tension or relief
- **Sensory Layering**: Add texture, temperature, and tactile details
- **Subtext**: What's not said is as important as what is (ominous silence, too-quiet areas)
- **Chekhov's Gun**: Every detail should serve the story or atmosphere
- Examples: "outline of a body slumped against the wall," "acrid smell of decay mingling with wet stone"

**4. Loot:** Action and payoff

- **Close-up Shot**: Intimate details that demand attention
- **Action Beats**: Specific, actionable moments that drive the scene forward
- **Emotional Stakes**: What the characters stand to gain or lose
- **Blocking**: Physical positioning and movement that tells a story
- **Props as Characters**: Objects that have personality and purpose
- **Reveal Moments**: The payoff for earlier foreshadowing
- Examples: "ancient stone altar stained with dark offerings," "goblin warrior clutching a bloodied spear," "ornate iron chest with intricate lockwork"

**5. Aside:** Tactical bypasses and scene connectors

- **Tactical Angles**: Ways to bypass the normal Far→Near→Close progression
- **Scene Connectors**: Direct paths from current scene to entirely different locations
- **The Sinister Left Hand**: Unexpected, potentially dangerous alternative routes
- **Hidden Shortcuts**: Secret passages that skip distance bands or connect scenes
- **Player Agency**: Multiple approaches that reward clever thinking and exploration
- **Narrative Bypasses**: Ways to skip exposition or jump to the action
- **Escape Routes**: Ways out that add tactical options and player choice
- Examples: "narrow tunnel bypassing the main chamber," "loose stone leading directly to the treasure vault," "tapestry concealing a shortcut to the surface"

**6. Stats:** Minimal creature/item statistics (AC, HD, Att, ML, Align) with location. Level 0 should be various small animals which are likely food for higher level creatures, and may be useful for foreshadowing at "far".

## Guidelines

### Writing Techniques

- Use the Royal Order of Adjectives (approximately 1 in 3 descriptive slots)
- **Show, Don't Tell**: Let players infer meaning from sensory details
- **Active Voice**: Use strong, specific verbs that create movement
- **Sensory Hierarchy**: Lead with the most impactful sense for the moment

### Theater of the Mind Techniques

- **Foreshadowing & Payoff**: Plant clues at Far/Near, reveal specifics at Close
- **Progressive Revelation**: Build suspense through layered discovery
- **Atmospheric Storytelling**: Prioritize mood and atmosphere over mechanics
- **Character Blocking**: Consider where everyone is positioned and why
- **Pacing Control**: Use rhythm, pauses, and timing to control tension

### Cinematic Techniques

- **Shot Progression**: Wide establishing → Medium character → Close-up detail
- **Lighting as Character**: Use light/dark to create mood and reveal information
- **Sound Design**: Layer ambient, environmental, and character sounds
- **Visual Metaphors**: Use shapes, colors, and composition to suggest themes
- **Subtext**: What's unsaid often carries more weight than what's spoken

### Game Techniques

- **Positioning:** Designate the position of each creature, trap, and treasure on this imaginary stage. Some might be found at "far" while others might only be revealed at "close".
- **Inform Player Agency**: Provide sensory details that allow players to make informed decisions. For example, if there is a dangerous monster, foreshadow its presence at Far/Near, but only reveal specifics at Close.
