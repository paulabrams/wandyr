# Alignment as Insight

In this variant, we use Alignment instead of Insight.

Players roll two (2) dice, but might roll three (3) dice for Advantage/Disadvantage (i.e. for best/worst of 2 out of 3).

- **Disadvantage**: If you do not have a relevant Alignment, roll with Disadvantage. ("Are you proficient?")
- **Advantage**: If you have a relevant Alignment, roll with Advantage if your Alignment Level is greater than the Difficulty Level. ("Are you better than the opposition?")
- **Normal**: Otherwise, roll two dice.

## Difficulty Level

The Difficulty Level is simply the monster level, trap level, etc. (calibrated to OSR Hit Dice — orcs are level 1). The Guide will tell the player the level upon asking, once they have interacted with the specific threat at hand.

- **Spells**: The Difficulty Level is the higher of the spell level and the nominal Difficulty Level.
- **Items**: The Guide may adjust the Difficulty Level for the approach and equipment at hand. A better tool lowers it (a rope makes climbing easier); a poor or missing tool raises it (an orc is level 1, but unarmed you might count as level 3). Some specialized tasks still require the right item — no lockpicks, no pick.

## Oracle Target

The Guide sets the Oracle target based on the overall chaos of the situation — mobs, pits, flames, spiky balls. This is the "room difficulty". This never includes the Difficulty Level of monsters or specific tasks in the situation.

## Sweet and Spicy

If your roll is 6+1 ("Sweet and Spicy"), your roll counts as successful but *appears unsuccessful*. You lose 2 Hearts, or only 1 if you apply extra Effort. However, this cannot drop you below 1 Heart. Fortune favors the bold.

- **Effort**: You can apply extra Effort unless you have Baggage, or are stealthy, climbing, swimming, wrestling, or making it look easy.

## Design Notes

### Design Tenets

- **Difficulty is Transparent**: In the standard rules, the Guide sets the Oracle Target, which combines multiple factors. When done right this makes things simple for the player, but it can easily drift into GM Fiat and inconsistencies. Since it's not transparent, players are not given information with which to make good decisions.
- **Unbounded fiction, clamped dice.** Room and monster levels are unbounded, but the worst dice state is Disadvantage and the highest target is 12. Nothing stacks. The intention is to buffer/bound the dice odds: better than the opposition → Advantage; no idea what you're doing → Disadvantage; otherwise roll normally — *even when outclassed*.
- **No double-counting.** Monster level affects only the dice; scene chaos affects only the target. A big monster raises the target only through the chaos it brings to the scene (fire, terror, minions), never through its level directly.
- **Items adjust the Difficulty.** Equipment and approach change the effective level — not the Oracle target. A rope lowers it; going unarmed against an orc raises it (level 1 → level 3). Proficiency (Alignment) and gear are separate questions: you can be trained and still outmatched by the situation. Specialized tasks may still require the right item outright (lockpicks). Combat gear is already priced elsewhere: weapons in damage dice, armor and shields in iron Hearts.
- **Proficiency is the dividing line.** Any relevant Alignment at Level 1+ means you are *proficient* — you know what you're doing, and you roll normally or better. Disadvantage isn't a penalty; it's what the world feels like without proficiency. The check is binary and visible to the whole table: either something on your sheet plausibly covers this, or nothing does — no magnitude to argue about. Default generous.
- **Sweet and Spicy is Effort's heir.** RAW Effort was you paying fortune (declared, visible strain); Sweet and Spicy is fortune paying you (automatic, hidden success). Luck hides its work; effort shows it — same family, opposite directions. Effort's old exclusion list survives in the pricing: fortune's bill is 2 Hearts, and bracing with extra Effort absorbs half — where you can't brace, you pay in full. Hearts remain the currency of boldness, same as spells and Extraordinary attempts.
- **Twists follow competence.** Counting only kept dice, Advantage trends Sweet (7.4% double Sweet, 0.5% double Spicy per roll) and Disadvantage is the mirror (7.4% double Spicy). Competence is safety; fumbling courts disaster.

### Problems Solved

- **"What's the current Insight?" (20× per session).** Insight was rotating shared state, and shared state decays — especially with players on Zoom or drifting. We tested the whole spectrum: per-roll Insight (adjudication after every throw, no way to plan), per-Turn with a public die (still 20 questions a session), per-Muster omen (static but arbitrary — rolled, not earned). Any *changing* Insight pays either a lookup cost or an adjudication cost. The fix is state that doesn't change during play: your Alignment is on your sheet, the threat's level is told once, and the question can't be asked because the answer never moves.
- **Oracle target fiat.** The target was a per-question Guide judgment combining hidden factors. Now every input is visible and announced before the roll: chaos prices the scene, items and approach ease the Difficulty Level, and the monster's level moved to the dice. Players can *fight* every input — thin the mob, pack the rope, raise your Alignment — which turns fiat into strategy.
- **Double-counting big monsters.** A high-level monster used to raise the target *and* loom over the ruling. Now its level counts exactly once (the dice); it only raises the target through the chaos it actually brings to the scene (fire, terror, minions).
- **Impossible rolls in an unbounded sandbox.** The world is unbounded but the dice are clamped: the worst state is Disadvantage, the highest target is 12, nothing stacks. Fighting Demogorgon is crazy — every roll still lands ~19% in a quiet room — but never mathematically impossible. The real danger lives in his Hearts and damage, where it belongs.
- **Players resist Disadvantage.** Threshold penalties get litigated at the table, and a rule applied grudgingly is just a recurring argument. So the penalties moved to things nobody argues with: the room (impersonal) and the untrained line (binary — either something on your sheet covers this or nothing does). Advantage, which players enforce eagerly and accurately, is the only per-character dice bonus.
- **The 6+1 collision.** Rolling a Sweet and a Spicy on the same throw was the twist economy's undefined case. Now it has a meaning: success that appears unsuccessful, at a cost in Hearts.
- **Where every Insight face went.** Trait, Skill, Byname, and Feat are the inputs Alignment Level already counts. Effort survives in the Sweet and Spicy pricing. Item adjusts the Difficulty Level (unarmed vs an orc: level 1 → level 3); combat gear was always paid out in damage dice and iron Hearts. Nothing landed back on the dice — only Alignment vs. Difficulty ever changes what you roll.

### Sweet and Spicy Examples

The whole table sees the 6+1 — the *characters* don't. The Guide narrates apparent failure; the truth surfaces when it matters.

- **The duel.** *The final duel between Inigo and the Count.* "I'm sorry, Father, I tried" — slumped against the wall, bleeding (1 Heart) — and then the hand tightens on the hilt.
- **The attack.** Your blade glances off the ogre's hide — or so it thinks. It found the gap; the wound just hasn't spoken yet. Its counter-swat clips you on the way out (1 Heart — you drove through with extra Effort).
- **The sneak.** The guard turns straight toward your shadow... holds... and walks past. You wrenched your knee staying statue-still (2 Hearts — no extra Effort while stealthy).
- **The climb.** Your hold tears loose and you swing out over the drop — the party gasps — then you haul yourself over the lip, shoulders on fire (2 Hearts — no extra Effort while climbing).
- **The haggle.** The merchant scoffs and turns her back... then mutters, "Half price. Get out before I think better of it." It cost you your pride (1 Heart).
