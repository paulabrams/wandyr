# Spicy Muster Effects

When rolling Hearts during Muster, if any die shows a 1, the result is **Spicy**—adding complications or stylistic flair. In adventures with ongoing threats, curses, or environmental dangers, rolling spicy during Muster can invoke special contextual effects that reflect the character's exposure to those dangers.

## Core Concept

A spicy roll during Muster doesn't just mean "bad luck." It can represent:

- **Early warning signs** of corruption or exposure
- **Psychological pressure** from recent encounters
- **Environmental influences** beginning to take hold
- **Foreshadowing** of dangers ahead

These effects should feel thematic and contextual to the adventure, not generic penalties.

## Example: Vault of First Light

In the Vault of First Light, characters are exposed to First Light gold—cursed treasure that causes memory overload and identity fracture. When rolling Hearts at Muster, a spicy result could indicate:

### Spicy Muster Effects (First Light Context)

**If a character rolls spicy (1) during Muster:**

They may experience early signs of corruption or psychological impact:

- **Memory Fragment:** Brief flash of a past life—a face, a name, a moment. The character knows this isn't their memory. (+1 to next Oracle roll if they embrace the vision; -1 if they resist it)
  
- **Gold Fascination:** The character feels drawn to inspect any gold items they're carrying. They must make a WIS save (DC 10) or spend a Turn examining gold, even if it's just ordinary coin.
  
- **Whispers:** Faint voices in an unknown language. They fade when the character focuses on them, but return in quiet moments. Other party members don't hear them.
  
- **Emotional Echo:** Sudden, intense emotion that doesn't match their current situation—grief for someone they've never met, joy at a victory they didn't win. Lasts a few minutes.
  
- **Language Slip:** The character uses a word or phrase from an ancient language they shouldn't know, or speaks with an accent they've never had. They don't realize until someone points it out.
  
- **Reality Glitch:** For a moment, the current scene feels like a dream—distant, unreal. The character questions whether this is their real life or a memory of someone else's.

### Progression

If a character rolls spicy multiple times during Muster (especially in consecutive sessions), the effects can escalate:

- **First spicy Muster:** Minor psychological effect (memory fragment, whispers)
- **Second spicy Muster:** More pronounced effect (language shift, emotional echo)
- **Third spicy Muster:** Signs of Stage 1 corruption (fascination, reality dissociation)
- **Fourth+ spicy Muster:** Consider advancing to Stage 2 effects

### Contextual Triggers

Spicy Muster effects can be more intense if:

- The character has **touched First Light gold** (even briefly)
- The character has **witnessed others affected** by the gold
- The character is **carrying items** from affected corpses
- The party is **deep in the Vault** (lower levels = stronger effects)
- The **celestial conjunction** is approaching

## General Guidelines

### For Any Adventure

When implementing spicy Muster effects:

1. **Make it thematic:** The effect should reflect the adventure's core threat or mystery
2. **Keep it narrative:** Prefer roleplay cues over mechanical penalties
3. **Escalate gradually:** One spicy roll = minor effect; multiple = progression
4. **Offer agency:** Let players interpret or respond to the effect
5. **Foreshadow:** Use spicy effects to hint at dangers without revealing everything

### When to Use

Consider using spicy Muster effects when:

- The adventure has an **ongoing environmental threat** (corruption, curse, disease)
- Characters have **recent exposure** to dangerous forces
- The adventure has a **psychological component** (fear, madness, memory manipulation)
- You want to **build tension** between sessions
- The adventure's **theme** supports it

### When NOT to Use

Avoid spicy Muster effects when:

- The adventure is purely physical/dungeon-crawl focused
- No ongoing threat exists
- It would feel like arbitrary punishment
- The table prefers pure mechanical resolution

## Examples from Other Adventures

### Cursed Location

**Spicy Muster:** Character has disturbing dreams about the place. They wake knowing something specific about the location (Oracle reveals one room's danger or secret).

### Plague/Contagion

**Spicy Muster:** Character shows early symptoms (cough, fatigue, strange mark). Not yet mechanically dangerous, but foreshadows the threat.

### Haunted Area

**Spicy Muster:** Character sees or hears something others don't. A ghost attempts to communicate, or the character feels the presence of the dead.

### Time Pressure

**Spicy Muster:** Character realizes they've lost time or had a gap in their memory. Something happened they can't remember.

## Implementation Notes

- **Player buy-in:** Introduce this concept at session start if using it
- **Oracle guidance:** Use the Oracle to determine if a spicy Muster effect manifests
- **Optional enhancement:** Let players choose to accept a spicy effect for a narrative benefit (e.g., "I'll take the memory fragment if it helps us understand the Vault")
- **Reversibility:** Most effects should fade or be curable if the threat is resolved
- **Documentation:** Track which characters have experienced spicy Muster effects for continuity

## Balance

Spicy Muster effects work best when they:

- Add flavor without punishing the player
- Create interesting roleplay opportunities
- Build atmosphere and tension
- Connect to the adventure's themes
- Can be resolved or overcome through play

Avoid effects that:

- Permanently disable characters
- Remove player agency
- Feel like arbitrary punishment
- Don't relate to the adventure
- Create bookkeeping burdens
