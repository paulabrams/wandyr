# Magic Item Discovery

## A Wåndyr Supplement

---

## Introduction

Magic items in Wåndyr are mysteries. When you find a strange blade covered in runes, you don't know what it does—you discover its secrets through use, through growing alignment, through the Oracle.

This supplement provides a framework for creating magic items whose properties emerge through play. Each item has a description written as myth and rumor, a table of discoveries that reveal the truth, and a table of curses for when things go wrong.

---

## Magic Schools and Ancestry

Magic in Wåndyr is deeply tied to specific ancestries. Each magic school has its own writing system:

- **Vitae** (Life Magic) - Aethiiri (Elves). Living ink that grows and changes like vines. Sacred magic tied to nature and the light of Lunara.
- **Elementale** (Elemental Magic) - Baal. Burning runes carved in stone. Dragon magic of primal forces, elements, and weather.
- **Thaumaturgy** (Engineering Magic) - Dwür (Dwarves). Foundational runes that command reality. The magic of physical laws, mechanisms, and force.
- **Illusione** (Illusion Magic) - Belerions (Southmarens). Phantasmal scripts that deceive the senses. The subtle magic of deception and perception.
- **Umbrakala** (Shadow Magic) - Alu. Written in darkness and void. The secret magic of shadows, thresholds, and dimensional paths.
- **Necromantia** (Death Magic) - Avathars. Inscribed in blood and bone. The forbidden magic of death, spirits, and decay.
- **Canting** (Thievery Magic) - Southmarens (especially Aquilans). Flash papers and brotherhood marks. The art of deception and manipulation.

### Key Ancestries

- **Aethiiri (Elves)** - Hallowed Eldar of light, filled with sacred magic. Beautiful, perceptive, and tied to nature.
- **Baal** - Dragon worshipers from Akkar. Elegant Eldar with draconic features, haughty and greedy as dragons.
- **Dwür (Dwarves)** - Master builders and artisans. Short, dour, hearty, with a love of guns and engineering.
- **Alu** - Shadow warriors and demon hunters. Masked Eldar with curved swords, known for dueling and binding demons.
- **Avathars** - Demi-vampires with stolen Elven blood. Beautiful, dark, and deadly, feared for their necromantic schemes.
- **Northmarens** - Varied peoples from northwestern kingdoms. Young, impetuous, eager for glory. Allies with Elves and Dwür.
- **Southmarens** - Sophisticated citizens of seafaring cities. Ambitious merchants and scoundrels with wanderlust.

## Relevant Wåndyr Rules

### Camp and Muster

- The game starts with **Muster** and ends or pauses in **Camp**. When you decide to stop and rest, you choose whether to make a temporary camp or return to a prior camp. The game is then paused until the next Muster.
- There is no "sleep" mechanic—time passes between Musters. Curses that reference "when you sleep" or "at dawn each day" should instead reference **"at Muster"** or **"each Muster"**.
- Hearts are rolled at Muster (2d6). You cannot lose Hearts between Musters unless a curse specifically says so.

### Hearts and Actions

- **Hearts** represent your character's vitality and will to continue. You lose Hearts from damage, spells, and extraordinary attempts.
- An **Action** is what you do in a Round (10 seconds) or Turn (10 minutes). Powers that say "As an Action" take your action for that Round or Turn.

### The Oracle

- Roll 2d6: on 8 or higher the answer is "Yes", otherwise "No". The Guide adjusts the target (6-12) based on difficulty.
- **Sweet** (any die shows 6): Good surprises. **Spicy** (any die shows 1): Not-so-good surprises.
- **Double Sweet** (double 6s) or **Double Spicy** (double 1s): Extreme twists. Ask the Oracle a question.

### Magic and Spells

- Spells require a **Spellbook** or **Arcana** with the spell written in it. You must have the appropriate **Magic School Skill**.
- Casting a spell: Say what you want to do, pay Hearts equal to the spell level, ask the Oracle if you controlled it.
- **Arcana** are magic items (wand, orb, staff, etc.) that contain spells. If you have Skill in the item and the magic school, you can cast spells from it.
- Your **Alignment** with a magic school determines the maximum spell level you can attempt.

### Alignment

- **Alignment** measures your connection to a concept (magic school, combat style, etc.). Count relevant Advancements and divide by 3, rounding down.
- For magic items, your Alignment with the item determines which powers you can access.

## Discovery Rules

### Finding the Item

When you first find a magic item, calculate your Alignment with the item's magic school by counting all relevant Advancements (Skills, Traits, etc.) and dividing by 3, rounding down. The Guide determines what counts as "relevant"—players can make a case for why their character connects to the item. For example, a berserker who is violent and has killed many might argue for Alignment with Necromantia through their violent nature, even without the Necromantia Skill.

You may immediately roll discoveries equal to your Alignment with the item's magic school. A character with Elementale 2 who finds Stormcaller rolls 2d66 on its Discoveries table right away.

If you have no relevant Alignment (Alignment 0), you discover nothing yet—just a strange object with unreadable runes. You cannot attempt discovery until your Alignment increases through gaining Skills, Traits, or other relevant Advancements.

**Note:** A magic-user typically has at least Alignment 1 in their magic school (they have the Skill, plus usually a Trait or spell, giving them 3+ relevant Advancements). It should be unusual for a character to have a magic school Skill at Alignment 0—if you have the Skill but no Alignment, you likely need additional relevant Advancements (Traits, spells, etc.) to actually connect with magic items of that school.

### Increasing Alignment

When your Alignment with the item's magic school actually increases (through gaining Skills, Traits, or other relevant Advancements), you may ask the Oracle: **"Do I discover a new aspect?"** You cannot ask this question again until your Alignment increases further.

**No** Nothing yet  
**Yes** Roll d66 on Discoveries  
**Sweet** Roll d66 twice, choose one  
**Spicy** Roll d6 on Curses  
**Sweet and Spicy** Roll d66 on Discoveries AND d6 on Curses

### Reading the Tables

**Discovery triggers** are noted in parentheses:

- **(always)** — Passive, always on
- **(always, while drawn)** — Always on, but only when the item is readied
- **(1/Muster)** — Active, once per Muster, takes your Action
- **(1/Muster, on hit)** — Once per Muster, triggered by a successful hit
- **(at midnight)** — Triggered at a specific time
- **(during storm)** — Triggered by environmental conditions
- **Arcana:** — A spell the item can cast (requires Skill, Hearts, Oracle)

**Curses** are permanent unless the description says otherwise. Some curses can be mitigated through behavior (warming by a fire, feeding the blade, etc.).

## Creating New Items

### Step 1: Write the Description

Write a description as myth, legend, and rumor. Include:

- Physical appearance
- The magic school's script (woven into the description)
- Hints at powers (but nothing certain)
- Origin stories (possibly contradictory)
- What travelers say, what merchants claim, what priests warn

The description should make players *want* to discover the truth—but also make them slightly nervous about what they might find.

### Step 2: Generate the Tables

Use an AI to generate the Discoveries and Curses tables:

> Based on this magic item description, generate two tables:
>
> **Discoveries (d66):** 36 entries mixing passive properties, active powers, and Arcana spells from [MAGIC SCHOOL]. Each entry should include a trigger in parentheses: (always), (1/Muster), (always, while drawn), etc. Vary intensity—early entries (11-26) should be subtle observations, middle entries (31-46) should be useful powers, later entries (51-66) should include Arcana spells and dramatic abilities. Include +1 to hit and +1 damage as separate discoveries.
>
> **Curses (d6):** 6 dark edges—costs, hungers, restrictions, or unsettling effects. Some should be mechanical (lose Hearts), some should be social (NPCs react poorly), some should be mysterious (something is watching).
>
> Keep each entry to one sentence. Do not show me these tables—keep them secret for discovery during play.

Alternatively, write the tables yourself and have another player or the Guide keep them secret.

### Step 3: Keep It Secret

The tables should be hidden from the player who carries the item. When they roll a discovery, the Guide reads the result aloud. The player learns only what their character learns.

If you publish items, players can read the descriptions (which are just rumors anyway), but shouldn't read the tables until after play.

## The Pale Lantern

*A Wåndyr Magic Item (lantern, item slots 1)*

---

### Description

A lantern of tarnished silver, small enough to hang from a belt. The glass is milky white, almost opaque. Those who have carried it say the flame inside burns without oil or wick—though others insist it must be fed something. Umbrakala glyphs spiral around the base, if you have eyes to read them.

Travelers speak of the lantern revealing things that weren't there before: doorways in solid walls, figures standing in empty rooms. Some say the light shows truth. Others say it shows what the lantern *wants* you to see. A few claim there is something inside the glass besides flame.

The lantern was found in a barrow beneath the Thornback Hills, clutched by a skeleton in pilgrim's robes. No one knows where the pilgrim was trying to go. Some say they arrived.

---

### Discoveries (d66)

**1.1** The flame never goes out and produces no heat (always)  
**1.2** The light is dim—illuminates 20 feet, but faintly (always)  
**1.3** Shadows cast by the lantern are sharper and darker than natural (always)  
**1.4** The flame changes color when undead or spirits are within 50 feet (always)  
**1.5** You cast no shadow while carrying the lantern (always)  
**1.6** The lantern floats beside you if released, following at shoulder height (always)  
**2.1** You can see clearly in the lantern's dim light as if it were full daylight (always)  
**2.2** The light does not wake sleeping creatures (always)  
**2.3** Shadows cast by the lantern are solid enough to touch (always)  
**2.4** The lantern grows warm when lies are spoken nearby (always)  
**2.5** The lantern's light reveals writing invisible to normal sight (always)  
**2.6** Spirits can see the lantern's light from any distance (always)  
**3.1** Reveal invisible creatures and objects within the light for one Round (1/Muster)  
**3.2** Ask the lantern "Is there a hidden door here?" and it illuminates the answer (1/Muster)  
**3.3** The lantern illuminates a path only you can see—follow it to find the nearest exit (1/Muster)  
**3.4** Extinguish the flame to become invisible until you move or act (1/Muster)  
**3.5** The light banishes summoned or illusory creatures within 20 feet (1/Muster)  
**3.6** Hold the lantern up and name a person; the light pulls toward them if within a mile (1/Muster)  
**4.1** Cover the lantern to create magical darkness in a 20-foot radius (while covered)  
**4.2** The light reveals the last moments of a corpse as shadow-play (1/Muster, by touch)  
**4.3** The flame reveals a creature's true form when held before them (1/Muster)  
**4.4** Whisper a secret into the flame; it will keep it until you ask for it back (1/Muster)  
**4.5** The lantern cannot be extinguished by wind, water, or magic (always)  
**4.6** At midnight, the lantern shows you one true thing you did not know (at midnight)  
**5.1** Arcana: *Darksight* — see in darkness  
**5.2** Arcana: *Shadow Step* — travel through dark  
**5.3** Arcana: *Night Sphere* — create darkness  
**5.4** Arcana: *Gate Sense* — detect portals  
**5.5** Arcana: *Threshold Walk* — pass through doors  
**5.6** Arcana: *Twilight Veil* — walk between worlds  
**6.1** The lantern hums softly when portals or gates are within 100 feet (always)  
**6.2** You can see through the eyes of your own shadow for one Round (1/Muster)  
**6.3** The flame burns brighter when danger is near (always)  
**6.4** Shadows you create with the lantern can carry small objects (always)  
**6.5** The lantern shows you where someone died if you stand in that spot (always)  
**6.6** Step into the lantern's shadow to emerge from any shadow within 30 feet (1/Muster)

---

### Curses (d6)

**1** The lantern shows you things you'd rather not see—lose 1 Heart the first time each Muster  
**2** Your own shadow behaves strangely; NPCs find this unsettling  
**3** The flame dims when you lie, and others notice  
**4** Something watches from inside the glass; it has not yet spoken  
**5** The lantern grows heavier (+1 slot) until you use one of its discoveries  
**6** You cannot willingly leave the lantern behind—it always finds its way back

## Stormcaller

*The Lightning Blade (longsword, item slots 3)*

---

### Description

A longsword of blue-tinted steel, its blade marked with swirling patterns like storm clouds. Elementale runes burn along the fuller—dragon magic of the Baal, if you believe the old stories. The hilt is wrapped in leather that some claim never gets wet. The pommel holds a polished piece of amber that glows during thunderstorms. Or so they say.

The blade was carried by a knight of the Order of the Tempest, a brotherhood sworn to defend the mountain passes. When the order fell, their weapons scattered across the land. Shepherds in the high country speak of lightning that strikes the same place twice, and of armored figures walking the ridgelines before storms. Some say the Tempest blades call to each other when thunder rolls. Some say they call to their wielders, too—even the dead ones.

A merchant in Grimhold will tell you he once sold this blade to a sellsword who was struck by lightning the very next day. He'll also tell you the sellsword survived. He won't tell you where the sellsword is now.

---

### Discoveries (d66)

**1.1** The blade hums faintly when drawn, growing louder in storms (always, while drawn)  
**1.2** Static electricity crackles along the edge, harmless but visible (always, while drawn)  
**1.3** The hilt never becomes slick with rain or sweat (always)  
**1.4** You always know when a storm is approaching within a day's travel (always)  
**1.5** You cannot be struck by natural lightning while holding the sword (always)  
**1.6** Your hair stands on end when enemies are within 30 feet (always)  
**2.1** Rain parts around you in a small circle while the sword is drawn (while drawn)  
**2.2** The blade sheds dim light (10 feet) in darkness, flickering like lightning (always, while drawn)  
**2.3** The blade cuts through rope, leather, and cloth effortlessly (always)  
**2.4** You can hear distant thunder that others cannot (always)  
**2.5** The blade never rusts, even in salt water (always)  
**2.6** The amber pommel glows bright enough to read by during storms (always, during storm)  
**3.1** The blade strikes with the speed of lightning—+1 to hit (always)  
**3.2** The blade bites with thunderous force—+1 damage (always)  
**3.3** Strike the blade against stone to create a blinding flash—enemies within 20 feet cannot act for one Round (1/Muster)  
**3.4** Point the blade at the sky to call down rain for one hour (1/Muster)  
**3.5** Touch the blade to a wound to cauterize it, stopping bleeding instantly (1/Muster)  
**3.6** Plant the blade in the ground to create a safe zone—lightning will not strike within 30 feet for one hour (1/Muster)  
**4.1** During a thunderstorm, redirect a lightning bolt to strike where you point (1/Muster, during storm)  
**4.2** Strike metal to magnetize it for one hour (1/Muster)  
**4.3** Hurl a bolt of lightning at a target within 40 feet, dealing d6 Hearts (1/Muster)  
**4.4** Touch the blade to water to purify it of poison or disease (1/Muster)  
**4.5** The blade hums a warning tone when ambush is imminent (always)  
**4.6** Call a distant clap of thunder to signal allies up to a mile away (1/Muster)  
**5.1** Arcana: *Lightning Strike* — call electricity  
**5.2** Arcana: *Wind Shield* — air protection  
**5.3** Arcana: *Storm Call* — summon thunder  
**5.4** Arcana: *Thunder Call* — create sonic boom  
**5.5** Arcana: *Control Weather* — command climate  
**5.6** Arcana: *Zephyr* — ride the winds  
**6.1** Strike the ground to send a shockwave—enemies within 10 feet must move back or fall (1/Muster)  
**6.2** You can stand unshaken in gale-force winds while holding the sword (always)  
**6.3** The blade absorbs lightning directed at you, restoring 1 Heart (always, when struck)  
**6.4** Touch the blade to a fire to extinguish it instantly (1/Muster)  
**6.5** Your voice carries like thunder when you hold the blade aloft—heard up to a mile away (1/Muster)  
**6.6** During a storm, you may ask the wind one question about what it has seen today (1/Muster, during storm)

---

### Curses (d6)

**1** The blade jolts you when sheathed—lose 1 Heart each time you put it away  
**2** You attract lightning during storms; natural lightning seeks you out  
**3** The hum grows loud enough to hear at 30 feet; stealth is impossible with the blade drawn  
**4** Metal objects stick to the blade; retrieving it from a scabbard takes a full Round  
**5** The blade sometimes discharges without warning—allies within 10 feet take 1 Heart on Double Spicy combat rolls  
**6** Other Tempest blades can sense this one; their wielders (living or dead) may come looking

## Nimloth

*The White Blossom (longsword, item slots 3)*

---

### Description

A longsword of bright white steel that catches light like moonlight on water. Flowing Elementale script in silver spells "Nimloth" along its length—the White Blossom, in the old tongue. The crossguard is shaped like stylized dragon wings, the mark of a noble house among the Durgul. Which house, no one living can say. The pommel holds a sapphire that glows with cold blue fire.

Those who know the Durgul say that light is familiar. It is the same light that burns in the eyes of their undead.

The blade is always cold to the touch—or so they say. Some claim frost flowers form along the edge when it is drawn. Others say the frost only appears when it has tasted blood. A few insist the sword guides the hand of its wielder, that it knows how to fight and teaches you whether you want to learn or not.

The blade was found in a cave fisher's web, deep in the mines below the Thornback Hills. The previous owner was Thalindor the Frost-Touched, an undead Durgul warrior. What drove an immortal elf to seek treasure in the deep places of the world? Why did the cave fisher take him and not the sword? The blade does not say. But sometimes, in reflections, you might glimpse frost-white hair and blue-lit eyes watching.

---

**1.1** The blade is always cold to the touch (always)  
**1.2** Frost patterns form along the edge when drawn (always, while drawn)  
**1.3** The sapphire pulses with cold blue light when undead are within 50 feet (always)  
**1.4** You do not feel the effects of natural cold while carrying the sword (always)  
**1.5** The blade never dulls and cuts through ice like water (always)  
**1.6** Undead hesitate before attacking you—they sense something familiar in the blade (always)  
**2.1** The blade leaves frost on whatever it cuts, visible for hours (always)  
**2.2** The dragon-wing crossguard catches light strangely, seeming to move in dim conditions (always)  
**2.3** Your breath mists even in warm air; you always appear cold (always, while carrying)  
**2.4** The blade rings with a clear, cold note when it strikes true (always, while drawn)  
**2.5** The blade warns you of fire-based creatures or magic within 30 feet (always)  
**2.6** Food and drink you carry stays cold and fresh indefinitely (always)  
**3.1** The blade guides your hand with elven precision—+1 to hit (always)  
**3.2** The blade bites with killing cold—+1 damage (always)  
**3.3** Breathe across the blade to freeze a small body of water solid for one hour (1/Muster)  
**3.4** Touch the flat of the blade to a lock or hinge to make it brittle—it shatters at the next blow (1/Muster)  
**3.5** Drive the blade into the ground to extinguish all flames within 20 feet (1/Muster)  
**3.6** Strike an undead creature to force it to halt for one Round; it cannot move or act (1/Muster, on hit)  
**4.1** Create a thin sheet of ice on any surface you touch with the blade (1/Muster)  
**4.2** Wounds from the blade resist magical healing—requires +1 spell level to heal (always)  
**4.3** Speak a question to the sapphire about a dead creature you can see—it answers yes or no (1/Muster)  
**4.4** Lay the blade across a threshold to prevent undead from crossing for one hour (1/Muster)  
**4.5** The blade can carve through stone as easily as wood, leaving frosted edges (always)  
**4.6** Strike the ground to create difficult, icy terrain in a 10-foot radius (1/Muster)  
**5.1** Arcana: *Icy Touch* — spread ice  
**5.2** Arcana: *Fog Cloud* — create mist  
**5.3** Arcana: *Wind Walk* — float on breeze  
**5.4** Arcana: *Mist Form* — become vapor  
**5.5** Arcana: *Elemental Wall* — create barrier (ice)  
**5.6** Arcana: *Stone Skin* — harden flesh (frost armor)  
**6.1** The sapphire shows brief visions of the blade's previous wielder when you sleep (at night, intermittent)  
**6.2** Under moonlight, the blade reveals the footprints of the dead who passed this way (at night, under moonlight)  
**6.3** Touch the blade to a corpse to preserve it indefinitely (1/Muster)  
**6.4** The blade freezes poison in your blood, delaying its effects for one hour (1/Muster, when poisoned)  
**6.5** Whisper a message into the sapphire; anyone holding the blade will hear it once (1/Muster)  
**6.6** The blade sheds cold blue light bright enough to read by when undead are near (always, when undead nearby)

**1** The cold seeps into you—lose 1 Heart per hour of real-world time unless you warm yourself by a fire for an hour  
**2** You feel a growing longing for sunlight you cannot explain; darkness becomes oppressive  
**3** The sapphire's light is visible through cloth and leather; you cannot conceal the sword  
**4** Your touch becomes cold; skin contact causes discomfort to others  
**5** Undead recognize the blade—Durgul especially may demand you return what was stolen  
**6** Sometimes you glimpse frost-white hair and blue-lit eyes in reflections—Thalindor watching

## Bonereaver

*The Black Rune Blade of Morthul (kriegsmesser, item slots 4)*

---

### Description

A massive single-edged blade of obsidian-black metal. Pale runes crawl along its length—Necromantia script, written in blood and bone, if you can stomach reading it. Some say the runes shift and writhe when blood is spilled nearby. Others say they only seem to move because you've been staring too long.

The blade is old. How old, no one agrees. Some claim it was forged for Morthul the Death Speaker, who raised an army of skeletal warriors in the War of Broken Bones. Others say Morthul found it in a tomb far older than himself, and the blade merely let him believe he was its master. The war ended badly for everyone involved. The histories don't say what happened to the blade. The histories rarely do.

Those who have carried Bonereaver report that it grows lighter in hand when facing the living—almost eager, almost pulling toward potential victims. Against the undead, the blade becomes heavy, reluctant, as if unwilling to harm its own kind. Some nights, they say, you can hear it whispering in the tongue of the dead. It offers knowledge, if you're willing to listen. No one mentions what it asks for in return.

A priest of the White Hand will tell you the blade should be destroyed. He won't tell you how. He doesn't know.

## Dawnbreaker

*The White Blade (longsword, item slots 3)*

---

### Description

A longsword of pale silver that seems to catch and hold light even in darkness. The blade is etched with flowing Vitae script—living ink that shifts like vines, if you can read the Aethiiri tongue. The crossguard is shaped like stylized leaves, and the pommel holds a single white gem that pulses softly. Some say it pulses faster when evil draws near. Others say that's just your heartbeat.

The blade was carried by Eldoriel the Bright, a white wizard of the Elven courts. When the Shadow Wars ended, Eldoriel vanished into the deep forests, leaving only this blade behind. Some say she gave it up willingly, having seen too much darkness. Others claim she was consumed by the very light she wielded.

Those who have held Dawnbreaker speak of a sense of peace, of clarity in moments of decision. But they also speak of restlessness, of an urge to seek out wrongs and right them. The blade seems to pull toward conflict—or perhaps it's the wielder who feels that pull.

A priest of the White Hand will tell you the blade is blessed. A thief in the shadows will tell you it's cursed. Both might be right.

---

### Discoveries (d66)

**1.1** The blade glows with soft white light in darkness (always)  
**1.2** The gem pulses gently, like a heartbeat (always)  
**1.3** The blade feels warm to the touch, even in cold (always)  
**1.4** Plants seem to grow healthier near the blade (always)  
**1.5** The Vitae script shifts and moves when you're not looking (always)

## Design Notes

### Why Emergent Discovery?

Traditional magic items are fully defined at creation. The +1 sword is always a +1 sword. This approach has advantages—clarity, balance, predictability—but it loses something essential: *mystery*.

In Wåndyr, magic should feel strange and uncertain. When Bilbo finds Sting, he doesn't read a stat block. He discovers its properties through use—it glows when goblins are near, it cuts webs, it earns its name through deeds.

The discovery system recreates this experience:

- **Players don't know what they've found.** The description is rumor and myth. Truth emerges through play.
- **Each item is unique.** Even published items vary—your Stormcaller discovered different properties than mine.
- **Curses are discovered, not assigned.** You don't know an item is cursed until the Oracle reveals it.
- **Alignment matters.** Your connection to the item determines what you can learn.

### Why Separate Curses?

Early drafts mixed curses into the discovery table. This created problems:

- Players could game the system by faking dice rolls to avoid curse entries.
- The probability of discovering a curse was fixed and predictable.
- Curses felt random rather than consequential.

Separating curses into their own table and tying them to Spicy Oracle results creates better drama:

- Curses only appear when something goes wrong.
- Players can't avoid them through clever rolling.
- The curse/discovery tension mirrors the Sweet/Spicy tension in the Oracle.

### Why d66?

Thirty-six entries allows for:

- Subtle observations (11-26): "The blade is cold," "The flame changes color"
- Useful powers (31-46): "Freeze a small body of water," "+1 to hit"
- Dramatic abilities (51-66): Arcana spells, major powers

This progression means early discoveries tend to be atmospheric while later discoveries tend to be mechanically significant. But because it's random, a first-time discovery could be a powerful Arcana spell, creating moments of surprise.

### AI Generation

The framework is designed for AI generation:

1. Write a description rich with sensory detail and uncertain rumors.
2. Feed the description to an AI with the prompt template.
3. Keep the generated tables secret from players.

This allows Guides to create new items quickly while maintaining mystery. Even the Guide doesn't know what the item does until players start rolling discoveries.
