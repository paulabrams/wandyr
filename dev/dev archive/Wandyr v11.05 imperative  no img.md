# Welcome to Wåndyr

**Wåndyr** is an adventure game about a band of adventurers wandering through a pulp fantasy world. The focus is on exploration, following rumors, discovering new places, finding magic, treasure hunting, camping, singing, storytelling, cleverly overcoming impossible odds, and coming back alive with tales to tell. In the spirit of The Hobbit, Wåndyr celebrates the journey of going there and back again.

The stories and songs of your characters emerge during play, filled with wonder and surprises that even the Guide doesn't anticipate.

## Sandbox

The Guide describes the world but doesn't create a story beforehand. The local area remains wide open and undefined, even if some aspects of the wider world are known. This "sandbox" style allows everyone at the table to discover the unexpected together.

The Guide uses dice rolls and random tables to determine what happens rather than telling a predetermined story. Each new discovery adds to the growing lore of your campaign world.

![Oracle illustration][image3]

## The Oracle

The **Oracle** answers any yes/no question instantly, often adding unexpected "sweet" or "spicy" flavor to keep adventures surprising. Both Guide and you consult the Oracle during play.

For deeper questions, the Oracle excels at "20 questions" to reveal mysteries piece by piece. The Guide should also use the random tables in the **Wåndyr World Oracle**.

Wåndyr is compatible with most Old School Roleplaying (OSR) games, but with fresh mechanics. Thus, the Guide has a wealth of material to augment the Oracle.

# Starting a Game {#starting-a-game}

Wåndyr is a game played with friends. Select one person to be the Guide; the other people are players.

## 1 \- Starting Location

The Guide describes the starting location for the adventure, such as a medieval tavern in a town on the borderlands. The Guide can place this into a larger fantasy world, or let that be a mystery for now.

## 2 \- Create Characters

You need three characters. Each character has an alias, Trait and Skill, for example: `Lindon, brave, poleax`

![Character creation illustration][image4]

## 3 \- Select a Rumor

The Guide then lists a few rumors that hint about troublesome events in the area or interesting places to explore. Choose a rumor to follow.

## 4 \- Muster a Character

Roll 1d6 Hearts for each of your three characters, then choose one to "Muster" (play). From now on, you play as your mustered character. The Guide gives new characters 1-4 basic "hand me down" items for weapons, armor, tools, spellbooks, etc.

## 5 \- Pack for the Journey

The Guide should provide a landmark or general directions so the path of the journey is clear, and provide or suggest basic travel supplies (e.g. food, water, traveling cloak). You can swap items amongst yourselves, but must leave unnecessary items behind with your other characters.

## 6 \- Embark

Start play on the road. You play your mustered character; the Guide handles everything else.

![Gameplay illustration][image5]

## Characters

Each character starts with one Trait and one Skill. Character Level equals the number of Traits.

Rook, cunning, knife

"Rook" is played as cunning (Trait), knows how to use a knife (Skill), and is 1st level.

### Traits

A Trait is a keyword that describes how your character is played.

Each Trait must be a single word, or common compound word (e.g. "rat-catcher", "horse-thief"). Traits can describe physical appearance, personality, quirks, habits, customs, idioms, approach to solving problems, etc. Traits can refer to a culture, homeland, or ancestry (human, elf, dwarf, etc.) or whatever helps you paint a picture. Good Traits make the character fun and distinctive to play.

### Skills

A Skill is something the character has learned, either an item proficiency or a knowledge. Examples: dagger, longsword, bow, lockpicks, shield, armor, type of animal, spellbook, wand, arcane staff, harp, bushcraft, etc.

Attempting to use specialized items or knowledge always requires a specialized Skill, such as "greatsword", "herbal medicine", or a magic school.

### Names

New characters are novices who have yet to earn experience, accomplish heroic deeds, or build a legend. If you accomplish these in play, your character can earn Names. Each Name can be a byname (title the character goes by), performance (song, story, ballad, poem, etc.), magic spell, or special ability (thief skill, berserker rage, etc).

## The Muster {#the-muster}

> Ok boys, check your kit, check your weapons, it's today. You get a pint and a chaser, no more. Let's go

During the Muster, you take on the role of one character adventuring today. You also decide who leads the party.

Roll 2d6 Hearts for each of your three characters, then choose one to "Muster" (play). From now on, you play as your mustered character. The Guide gives new characters 1-4 basic "hand me down" items for weapons, armor, tools, spellbooks, etc..

## Party Hearts

During the Muster, elect a party leader. You have Party Hearts equal to the leader's Character Level (number of Traits). Party Hearts can be used in place of character Hearts.

### 10 Item Slots

Your character has 10 Item Slots to hold gear they have "equipped" and can access and use easily.

Each slot can hold one small item (cloak, flask, tinderbox, flask, 1 day rations, torch, coin purse, arrow quiver, lockpicks) or a bundle of tiny identical items (bowstrings, gems).

Larger items use 1-4 Item Slots:

- **Weapons:** dagger 1, sword 2, halberd 3, pike 4
- **Bows:** arrow quiver 1, bow 2, crossbow 3, arbalest 4
- **Shields:** buckler 1, heater 2, round shield 3, tower shield 4
- **Armor:** gauntlets 1, helm 2, gambeson 3, haubergeon 4
- **Musical Instrument:** pipe 1, lute 2, guitar 3, war drums 4

### Baggage

You can carry gear outside of your 10 Item Slots but must keep a separate list called "Baggage" (big bags or rucksacks). Accessing an item from Baggage takes d6 Turns. Baggage halves movement and causes safety issues when swimming, leaping, climbing, etc.

### Home Base

Your characters are part of an "adventuring party" with a "home base". They might be an adventurers guild, warband, canting crew, etc. Your base could be a tavern, flat house, hideout, etc. Describe and design your bases for fun.

### Fast-Travel

Bases enable fast-travel between established locations, skipping mundane journeys. The Guide can have bases generate rumors on their own, adding new leads between sessions.

![Camping illustration][image7]

## Camping {#camping}

In Wåndyr, each game starts with a Muster and ends in Camp. When you decide to stop and rest, you must choose whether to make a temporary camp or return home.

### Downtime

Camping is downtime. Each character exits play until the next time they Muster. The game is essentially "paused", allowing you and the Guide to openly and rapidly discuss topics that might be more difficult to do when actively playing a character. For example, at this time the Guide awards XP and you can spend XP. The party may also fast-forward in time.

### Comforts

The comforts of home are cheerful companions. A wheel of cheese, a crusty bread, mead, wine, or a good smoke can make even the most dismal dungeon feel fine.

If your character consumes a Comfort during Muster, they receive up to 2 extra Hearts. A 1-Heart comfort is 10 gp and uses 1 slot; a 2-Heart is 100 gp and 2 slots. Comforts are available in taverns, inns, and provisioners, or might be found.

# Advancement {#advancement}

New characters are unproven, untested novices with no prior experience, heroic deeds. *All this must develop during the game*.

## Experience {#experience}

Your character can earn Experience Points (XP) from their adventures. You can earn 1-6 XP for following your currently selected rumor to its full conclusion. You can also earn 1 XP by discovering an important new location or magical feature, mapping a new region, experimenting with magic, or rescuing captives. *Slaying monsters and looting treasure never directly earns XP.*

### Spending XP

In Camp, you can spend XP to buy Traits, Skills, and Names. Simply, gaining a new thing costs XP equal to the new number of that kind of thing. For example, gaining the 1st Name costs 1 xp, and gaining the 3rd Trait costs 3 xp.

### Earning Names

Your character is only eligible to take a Name after accomplishing a significant deed during play. For example, your character must cast a spell from a book *with Sweet* before they can learn it. After buying it with XP, you can either keep the Name for your character or assign it to an item (creating a "Named Item"). *It can be fun to hire an artisan to inscribe an item with its Names and decorations (inlays, etchings, gems, gold leaf, etc.).*

## Character Level {#character-level}

Your character has a Level, which is your number of Traits. 

The Guide uses character Level for setting the Oracle target. If the characters have a “party leader” the Guide can use their Level instead.

# Game Play {#game-play}

The game mostly consists of a conversation around the table where people ask questions.

When you ask a question about a *fact* yet unknown, the Guide asks the Oracle:

- "Is this town on a waterway?"  
- "Is there a trail in the woods?"  
- "Do they seem friendly?"

When you take action, you or the Guide asks the Oracle:

- "I flee and climb a tree\!"  
- "I throw my spear"  
- "I parry with my shield\!".

## Ask the Oracle {#ask-the-oracle}

Speak a question such as "does my spear hit the foe?" and roll 2d6: on 8 or higher it's "Yes", otherwise "No".

### Advantage/Disadvantage

With Advantage, roll 3d6 and keep the two best dice (on Disadvantage keep the two worst). Never remove a 1 from the roll (e.g. 1, 1, 6 \= 8).

Advantage can be gained with clever play or **Insight**.

A Disadvantage may apply for being unarmed, encumbered, poor position/visibility, using a bow in melee, etc. Disadvantage cancels Advantage, simply roll 2d6.

### Easy / Hard

The Guide sets the Oracle target number as easy (6), normal (8), hard (10), or extreme (12). When possible, the Guide should compare character Level with the Level of an opponent, monster, or challenge. *The Level of the party leader can be used instead; hopefully that's better\!?*

### Sweet and Spicy

When any die rolls a 6, the outcome is "Sweet," adding a stylistic flair. Double 6s indicate a bonus or benefit. Conversely, if any die rolls a 1, the outcome is "Spicy," also adding style. Double 1s signify a real penalty or complication.

Example: An archer snipes a sentry. On "spicy" another guard notices; on "sweet" the uniform is spotless.

The Guide can describe these effects or allow you to provide your own interpretation. Unless the side effect is straightforward and minor, ask the Oracle if it happens. *You must spend a Heart to attempt any kind of superhuman/magical stunt.*

## Time {#time}

Most of the game is a loose conversation between you.

The Guide tracks 10-minute "Turns" using the "Turn Clock". This is a glass or metal bowl in the middle of the table. The Guide loudly tosses a die into the clock each time you do something time-consuming. When the bowl has six dice the Guide empties it and rolls the dice, looking for 1's, which indicate a complication of some kind. The Guide also rolls the Turn Clock whenever you do something reckless.

*The Turn Clock is just for slow-time, so it is paused during fast-paced situations such as combat.*

![Time illustration][image8]

## Music {#music}

*Music is important because it makes everything better.* Your character can perform once per Muster (1/day) for each music instrument **Skill** and song **Name**. A performance can influence NPC reactions or raise party morale. After 1 Turn (10 min.) ask the Oracle if the performance has lifted spirits; each person in the audience *recovers* 1 Heart (up to Hearts at Muster).

Each performance reaches an audience size up to the total of the Name level, instrument slots, and possibly instrument quality ("lute \+1")  Higher level or group performances are more fun and may have additional benefits*.*

## Names (#names)

Names are helpful for Insight and influencing NPCs. You can also spend 1 Heart to reenact the feat when your character earned the Name.

### Named Items

A **Named Item** has at least one Name (at most one per item slot). If equipped at all times, you can use each Name once per Muster (1/day) at no Hearts cost.

## Mågįc

Wåndyr is full of magic, and much of the game is about exploring and meddling with magical secrets.

All magic in Wåndyr involves *True Names*, either written or spoken as words of power. A True Name can provide a magic enhancement to an item, a sorcery to bind a spirit into a “sentient” item, a sigil for a magic-user spell, and much more.

All magic items and magical objects bear mystical inscriptions: sharp dwarven runes, flowing elven script, or shifting arcane glyphs, only decipherable by *magic-users*.

### Magic Items {#magic-items}

Most magic items function as their mundane counterparts plus a magical enhancement. Elven boots silence footsteps, a hero's belt bestows strength, and a magic shield is more protective. If you're skilled in the item (i.e. with a Skill in the item), you can usually discover its function through use.

![Magic illustration][image9]

#### Sentient Items

Sentient items contain a bound soul of a magic-user, elemental, *d'jinn, ifr'it, kha'din*, dragon, eldar, deep one, or other entity. Bound spirits are notoriously capricious, jealous, and unhinged from confinement, their original dreams and ambitions maybe twisted by madness.

Sentient items are temperamental; when you first attempt to use one, ask the Oracle if the item \*accepts\* you. On Sweet or Spicy, the item also talks to you\!

If you carry a sentient item, your character grows in *alignment* and may discover deeper mysteries. You might take a personal **Name** such as "Wielder of Duskbringer".  It's petty, but they *hate* anyone who offends their fragile egos, such as carrying them after being rejected, refusing to follow their agenda, abandoning, or "cheating" on them with a rival item.

### Magic-Users {#magic-users}

Magic is classified by “magic school”, such as Vitae (life), Elementalis, Thaumaturgy (control), Illusione (illusions & mental), Umbrakala (shadows & gates), or Necromantia.

If your character has a **Magic School**, they are a "magic-user" literate in the magic language of that school. Words are magical, and magic is formed by magic words, runes, glyphs, arcane script, etc.

#### Spell Casting

Casting a spell requires using a Spellbook. This uses both your hands, you must say the magic words loudly and precisely, and must spend at least 1 Heart.

Expending 1 Heart casts the spell as a basic cantrip or "1st level" spell. You can boost the spell by spending Hearts up to your level in the magic school (the number of spells known in that spell school).

![Magic items illustration][image10]

#### Magic Oracle

Ask the Oracle if you cast the spell with intended results. A "NO" is not a failure; the magic had a complication rather than simply matching your intention.

#### Spell Duration

Each spell can be cast with a duration of either *Round* or *Turn*:  
**Round** **spells** start and finish instantaneously.  
**Turn** **spells** do not start instantaneously, but last for the current scene (e.g. a combat encounter or cutscene) or 10 min.

#### Learning Spells

A magic-user can *learn* a spell from a spellbook after casting it with *Sweet.* Upon paying 1 XP they gain the spell as a **Name** and no longer need the book to cast the spell. If they muster with the book they can cast once per Muster (1/day) at no Hearts. Alternatively, the magic-user may enchant an item, creating an **Arcana**. However, all this is undone if anyone else ever casts the spell from the spellbook.

#### Arcana

An Arcana is an item with one or more True Names of spells written on it. Arcana can be any kind of item (even tattoos) with magic-user "drip" based on the spell Names. If equipped at Muster, a magic-user can *ostentatiously* use each Name in an Arcana once per Muster (1/day), at no Hearts cost..

#### Magic Components

A Magic Component reduces this cost by 1 Heart per item slot consumed. *Magic components can be found in strange magical places.*

## Invocations {#invocations}

Otherworldly beings can be called on by speaking their *True* *Names*.

### Lore

Randomly generate new names. You may ask the Oracle "have we heard of this being before?", "is this being aligned with Law?", etc. Anything learned becomes part of the known Lore of the being.

### Invocations

You can make an Invocation by loudly and formally calling the True Name of the being *thrice* and stating a request. Ask the Oracle if you feel the being notices the request (on Sweet the being *likes* the request and on Spicy they *dislike* it).

If the being notices the request, the Guide follows existing Lore and asks the Oracle what the being does, if anything, and when, how, etc. The personality of the being and alignment of your character to the being matter a great deal.

### Alignment

Upon making an Invocation, your character loses d6 Hearts and XP. The being spends the XP to bring your character further into alignment with them (ask the Oracle what happens).

### Relics

Relics are sacred objects that serve as conduits to otherworldly beings. They can have Names, and speaking a Name with correct alignment activates a sacred power once per Muster (1/day). Similar to a sentient item, the being might also speak a Name. These Names are often oral tradition rather than written on the item, so you may need to learn Lore about the being to discover the Names.

## Monsters {#monsters}

Each monster (or trap, etc.) has a **Level** comparable to **Character Level**. The Guide sets the Oracle to “hard” if this is higher than Character Level; or “easy” if lower. Use the party leader's Character Level ("Party Level") when working together.

**Level 0:** giant rat, vermin, goblin  
**Level 1:** orc, skeleton, deathsquito  
**Level 2:** zombie, ghoul, giant ant, slime  
**Level 3:** wight, harpy, giant spider  
**Level 4:** werewolf, ogre, huge slime  
**Level 5:** mummy, minotaur, owlbeast  
**Level 6:** troll, wyvern, spectre, manticore  
**Level 7:** griffon, djinni, vampire  
**Level 9:** chimera, giant  
**Level 10:** golem, dragon

For more detail, the Guide can use OSR monsters or wing it as follows.

Weak pack/tribal monsters have d6 Hearts; leaders 2d6. A solitary “apex predator” monster has 2d6 or 3d6 Hearts per HD, multiple attacks, plus a deadly attack of 2d6 or 3d6, or d6 Hearts per Turn for HD+2 Turns (for paralysis, poison, petrification, etc).

## Fighting {#fighting}

In each Turn, you can Move and Attack (or take another action).

## Move

You can stand up OR move 40' (40 feet). Each impediment (e.g. Baggage, readying weapons, bad terrain, darkness) cuts this in half.

## Attack

When you attack, ask the Oracle if you "hit". Each "hit" deals d6 Hearts. More lethal weapons may have a "plus" bonus, e.g. "greatsword \+1". Attackers are open to counterattacks.

## Defend

When you are attacked or otherwise endangered, ask the Oracle if you avoid getting "hit". Armor and shields give 1 "iron" Heart per item slot, usable only against weapons. More protective armor may have a "plus" bonus, such as "shield \+1".

When two opponents attempt melee attacks against each other, reduce hits to each other by 1 Heart.

## Initiative

The Guide determines Turn order based on the situation, e.g. characters near the front of the party might be called before those in the rear, or characters who need to move to see what is happening.

Melee attacks go first and are usually simultaneous. However, when enemies approach from a distance, the weapon with the better reach (by item slots) has the "right of way" to attack first. Conversely, in close quarters combat (less than 10 feet of fighting space per person) the smaller weapon attacks first.

## Hearts in Combat

Hearts act as plot armor; your character is *not injured* while they have Hearts. A character at 0 Hearts is *injured* but can Camp and recover. A character below 0 Hearts is *dying* and needs magic healing within 1 Turn (10 minutes).

## Saving Throws

When you are exposed to a special effect (poison, petrification, breath, magic, paralyzation, etc.), ask the Oracle if you somehow avoid the "worst half" of the effect. On YES you take half the effect.

## The Oracle {#the-oracle}

Ask a question and roll 2d6: if the total is 8 or higher the answer is “Yes”, otherwise "No".

Always phrase the question for yes/no where the characters *want* to hear “Yes”:

- “Do we find shelter?”  
- “Is the sword magical?"  
- "Do I hit the monster?"  
- "Do I avoid the trap?

### Sweet & Spicy

When any die rolls a 6, the outcome is "Sweet," adding a stylistic flair. Double 6s indicate a bonus or benefit. Conversely, if any die rolls a 1, the outcome is "Spicy," also adding style. Double 1s signify a penalty or complication.

## Insight {#insight}

Insight is an opportunity to claim advantage. Roll d6 for Insight each Turn to determine the current Insight (1-6). If you use the option matching the current Insight on your roll, you gain Advantage on that roll.

### 1 \- TRAIT

Describe your approach using your Traits.

### 2 \- SKILL

Use a Skill from a helpful item (requires the item).

### 3 \- ITEM

Use a helpful item. Special items require a specialized Skill (e.g. longbow, lockpicks, greatsword, spellbooks, wand, etc.).

### 4 \- NAME

Use a relevant Name (byname, title, named item, or spell).

### 5 \- MORALE

You have Advantage if the group has one or more Party Hearts. The party is not out of morale.

### 6 \- EFFORT

Apply more effort. Not possible when carrying Baggage, maintaining stealth/subtlety, climbing, swimming, wrestling, or making something look easy.
